## Current war status

Day 157 turned on the strike that did not come. After a week of US-media reports that a US-Israeli offensive on Iran's energy and nuclear sites was imminent, President Trump abruptly called the attack off over the weekend, saying Iran and "other Middle Eastern countries" had asked Washington "to hold off any attack" because "the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to," and that Israel had agreed to join the pause (Semafor, Washington Post, NPR, Bloomberg). It is the first genuine de-escalatory inflection since the Day 154 collapse — and, per §3.5.3, the most Day 50 moment of the war: a presidential progress claim advanced ahead of any verification, with no text, no Iranian confirmation, and no verification mechanism named. The skeptical counter arrived almost at once: Trump tied the hold to "rapidly" reopening the Strait of Hormuz, yet Iran and Oman were discussing a new shipping route that Tehran said would not reopen the strait to pre-war terms; there was no sign of a breakthrough in the capital; and Israel said it remained on high alert (Washington Post, Bloomberg). FM Araghchi spent the weekend telling the Turkish, Pakistani and Saudi foreign ministers that Iran would answer "any adventurous action" with an "eye for an eye" (Global Times). Beneath the diplomacy the maritime front stayed kinetic: a Qatari-LNG carrier, the Gaslog Shanghai, was struck by an "unknown projectile" off Oman's Musandam Peninsula, its engine room damaged though without casualties (UKMTO, Bloomberg). The Houthi embargo of Saudi Arabia held at Bab el-Mandeb, with Sunday strikes on Red Sea oil depots cutting traffic to ~11 vessels; Brent held near its $90 weekend close. Nuclear bounded — IAEA no verification since Feb 28. Direction eases to mixed on the pause; 7-day risk critical; spillover critical; 30-day ceasefire probability ticks to 11. Day 157 = forty-seventh day of the 60-day window.

## Diplomatic state

Day 157 produced the war's first Washington-initiated pause since the Day 154 collapse, but on terms no party has yet agreed. Trump called off the imminent strikes citing an agreed "deal perimeter" and Gulf and "other Middle Eastern" appeals to hold off; the pause is pledged on rapidly reopening Hormuz. Yet the deal claim contradicts itself in public: Iran and Oman are discussing a route Tehran says would not reopen the strait, there is no breakthrough in the capital, and Araghchi is warning of an "eye for an eye" answer to "any adventurous action." Per §3.5.3 this is process moving ahead of agreement — a Day 50-style progress claim whose base rate (three prior reversals) argues for skepticism. What is new is that Gulf states now function as an active brake on Washington (coalition-cohesion clock) and Israel has, for now, agreed to the pause while staying on high alert.
