## Current war status

Day 200's dominant item was retrospective. The New York Times reported that July's attacks on three commercial ships in Hormuz — the strikes that collapsed the Islamabad MOU and restarted the blockade — were launched with no advance knowledge or authorisation from Pezeshkian, IRGC commander-in-chief Ahmad Vahidi, or the SNSC; Pezeshkian was surprised and called Vahidi, who disclaimed both. Iranian investigations traced the order to Hossein Taeb, cleric, ex-IRGC intelligence chief, now head of the Basij, an opponent of the deal from the start. Mojtaba Khamenei has not appeared publicly in ~6 months (Times of Israel, JPost). Tehran has neither confirmed nor denied. Two of three regional brakes came off in the same cycle: the Saudi-led coalition spokesman vowed a self-defence response to Saturday's Houthi projectile into Jazan's Al-Tuwal (2 wounded, mosque and buildings damaged), and a source familiar with Saudi thinking told CNN Riyadh is planning to retaliate and has briefed allies; Houthi spokesman Saree claims the target was a Saudi base at Sharurah, Najran. Only Baghdad de-escalated — reopening two of three Iran crossings and admitting Iranian investigators to the Petroline probe after seizing the Maysan launch platform. Sunday's unattributed Hengam Island strike was identified as the Panama-flagged products tanker **El Gaia**, 14 Indian crew, 13 rescued, 1 missing — revising the IRNA account of 1 killed (Jamshid Rajabi) / 4 wounded of ten aboard; India's MEA condemned it. Brent settled **$107.54** (+2.9%, from $104.47, high $108.40) on the Salalah vacuum, the shut Petroline and the SNSC restating that Hormuz stays closed until Washington "corrects its behaviour." Direction escalating; 7-day risk extreme; spillover critical; 30-day ceasefire probability holds at 2.
