## Current war status

Day 179 is the day the economic war stopped being a promise and became designations. Treasury Secretary Bessent unveiled the isolation plan as "Operation Economic Outcast," blacklisting nearly 60 people, businesses, and vessels and expanding the categories for secondary sanctions across shipping, oil, gold, crypto, aviation, and technology; he called it "an economic onslaught against Iran's financial connections around the globe" and said Iran faces "two paths: complete global isolation and a subsistence economy, or a path back to normalcy" (NBC News, CBS News, The Hill, NPR, Fox Business, Washington Post). But the pillar the plan was built to break was left standing: no major Chinese bank or yuan-clearing rail was named — Washington confined its China measures to the already-sanctioned teapot refineries — and Bessent's "no one is above the reach of U.S. sanctions" stayed a warning rather than a designation (CNBC, Fortune). Beijing answered by invoking its 2021 anti-sanctions blocking statute for the first time, ordering Chinese firms not to comply and repeating that the sanctions are "illegal unilateral" acts with "no basis in international law" (Global Times, China-Global South Project, IRNA). Iran answered with rhetoric and maritime pressure: new SNSC secretary Rezaei vowed a "seismic" response and claimed ~70 million barrels shipped despite the blockade, Araghchi called the plan a "desperate" ploy set to fail, and Tehran renewed its threat to fine, detain, or confiscate vessels defying its Hormuz protocols (CNBC, CNN). The market sold the news: Brent slipped below $92 as traders judged the deferred China-bank collision to be less than the maximal shock; CENTCOM enforcement rose to ~55 ships rerouted / 3 disabled / 2 boarded since mid-July. Direction holds mixed (the plan landed as designations but spared the China collision it was engineered to force); 7-day risk critical; spillover critical; 30-day ceasefire probability holds 5.
