## Current war status

Day 196 was the day the war acquired a date — set in Washington, not Tehran. Pressed on the fighting he has spent weeks calling "small potatoes," President Trump said he is "not looking for a deal with Iran" and does not expect oil prices to fall until "right after" the November midterm elections, publicly foreclosing the near-term off-ramp and tying relief to the electoral calendar as US retail gasoline hit a Labor Day record (UPI, CNBC, NBC News). The market repriced at once: Brent jumped to $105 and touched roughly $107 intraday — its highest since May, a near-6% session gain from the ~$101–102 of Day 195 — with WTI up about 6% toward $102 and the spike now flagged as a live input to a possible US interest-rate increase (Al Jazeera, NBC News, Whatfinger). The Saudi–Yemen front Day 195 had opened turned to mass-casualty ground war: government-aligned forces counterattacked in Taiz and Hodeidah as the Houthis pressed an offensive toward Bab al-Mandeb, with hundreds reportedly killed in twenty-four hours, the Houthis alleging 54 Saudi airstrikes in twelve hours and a prison strike that killed seven, fresh Houthi strikes on Aramco sites at Abha, Najran and Jizan, and UN Yemen envoy Grundberg warning of a slide into full-scale war (Al Jazeera, NPR, CNN). The nuclear file left the shadows: after more than a year of "complete blindness," the IAEA referred Iran to the UN Security Council, with Tehran conditioning access on a final deal (CNN, FDD). The Hormuz "restricted zone" gained coordinates from Chabahar into the Gulf of Oman and Arabian Sea, and the major-Chinese-bank sanction stayed pinned behind the September 24 Xi summit (GlobalSecurity/IRNA, Newsweek, CNBC). Direction holds escalating; 7-day risk extreme; spillover critical; 30-day ceasefire probability slips to 2.
