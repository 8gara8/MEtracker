# Day 265 brief — Iran answers Qeshm with drones at Kuwait as the Houthis fire on Riyadh; ADNOC crew hurt in the strait; Qatar warns diplomacy is at risk

*Thursday, November 19, 2026 · Day 265 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran answered Tuesday's US strike on Qeshm with fire at a second host state, and the Houthis widened the arc to Riyadh. Iran's Army said its drones hit Patriot batteries and launch platforms at Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait early Wednesday, the first Iranian attack on Kuwait since September by the Times of Israel's count; Kuwait said its air defences engaged hostile drones, reported no damage and condemned a "dangerous escalation." Bahrain reported another interception, and Gulf News logged a further launch at a US base in Kuwait early Thursday, again with no damage reported. Iran's foreign ministry called the attacks self-defence and warned neighbours to stop Washington using their territory. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted Houthi cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh and struck 60 Houthi targets; Houthi-run health officials reported at least 17 killed. At sea, ADNOC said 11 crew were injured and one is missing after an attack on one of its vessels in the strait, while Iran's army claimed three more tankers hit mines. Qatar condemned the attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain as a danger to diplomacy even as Iranian and Qatari officials met in Oman. NBC reported near-daily US-Iranian exchanges, most undisclosed, and Reuters sources said Mossad chief Roman Gofman lobbied Washington for fresh joint strikes; a White House official said major combat is unnecessary. Rubio saw no "imminent threat" from the reported dirty-bomb plot. hormuz.now counts 1.6 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis; Brent fell 1.9% to about $91.4. The Shenzhen trilateral meets today. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases to 7.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The loop closes: Qeshm is answered in Kuwait, and the Houthis add Riyadh.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock has turned from an exchange into a cycle. Day 264's reading was that a proportional, casualty-free US strike on Qeshm left neither side anything to avenge. Iran replied anyway, and chose a second host rather than a US platform: its Army says drones hit Patriot batteries and launch platforms at Ali Al-Salem in Kuwait, the first Iranian attack there since September by the Times of Israel's count. Ebtesam Al Ketbi of the Emirates Policy Center, quoted by Gulf News, named the pattern: the United States strikes Iran, but Iran "strikes the Gulf instead." Ceilings still hold. Kuwait and Bahrain report no damage, CENTCOM reports interceptions, and Tasnim's claim of hits is unverified. Three things widened in 24 hours. Kuwait moved from unattributed alert to named target. Iran's foreign ministry now addresses the hosts directly, urging them to stop Washington using their territory, while deputy speaker Hamidreza Haji Babaei names bypass pipelines and ship-to-ship transfers as targets, the bypass-as-target prior stated outright. And the Houthis fired cruise missiles at Riyadh as the coalition struck 60 targets in Yemen, with at least 17 killed by Houthi-run health officials' count. NBC's report that most US strikes over two months went undisclosed means the visible rhythm understates the real one. ADNOC's 11 injured crew end a run of cycles with no adopted casualty change. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases from 8 to 7.

**2. Mediators keep meeting while warning that the attacks are undoing their work.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock is now running against its own sponsors' warnings. Qatar's foreign ministry called the attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain "a dangerous escalation that threatens to complicate efforts aimed at containing tensions," and spokesman Majed Al Ansari, per the Times of Israel, called escalation an "extremely grave danger" to an agreement. Yet the same report places Qatari and Iranian officials in Oman on Tuesday, and Araghchi says the Oman talks aim to prevent renewed fighting and build a regional security framework without foreign military involvement, a framework the hosts under fire have not endorsed. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, via Press TV, says the two-day plan is "on the table" and Washington "must now respond." The sequencing wall is unchanged: Gulf News reports Iran wants the blockade lifted, assets freed and exports restored first, while Washington wants verifiable nuclear steps from the start. Qatar's reported window closes around 24 November, five days away, still with no named US negotiator. Washington's signals split. A White House official says economic pressure makes major combat unnecessary, and Rubio sees no imminent threat from the dirty-bomb reporting. Israel pulls the other way: Reuters sources say Mossad chief Roman Gofman lobbied for joint strikes and that Netanyahu sees the window narrowing, though the IDF reportedly judges no imminent threat. The caretaker-risk prior strengthens. China's Guo Jiakun previewed Shenzhen as an exchange on "global peace" without naming Iran; Iraq's Jurf al-Sakhr talks stalled with Esmail Qaani in Baghdad.

**3. Taiwan: oil falls to a three-month low as the strait empties further and gas stays shut.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 11 tanker crossings in seven days, 1.6 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and down from 2.0, with 40 commercial ships in total and no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days (one of each in 30). PortWatch puts tanker deadweight at 0.8% of normal to 15 November; Kpler's preliminary estimate of about 8.3 mb/d, which counts dark transits, still dates from the week to 11 November. Brent fell 1.87% to about $91.43 on Wednesday, its lowest since 31 August and about 20% down this month, with Trading Economics citing a 3.38 million-barrel US crude build and expectations that Washington will avoid full-scale war. That is the oil-priced-as-surplus prior at its starkest: physical flow through the strait falls while the price falls with it. For Taiwan the binding constraint is gas, not crude. No LNG moves through Hormuz, and on this file's record QatarEnergy's force majeure runs to end-December for some buyers. Three marginal risks grew. An attack on an Emirati-operated vessel injured 11 crew, which bears on war-risk premiums and crewing for substitute cargoes. Haji Babaei's threat against bypass pipelines and ship-to-ship transfers targets exactly the routes that replace Hormuz volumes. Kuwait, a northern Gulf exporter, is now a named target. The Shenzhen trilateral today is the next East Asian read; the Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
