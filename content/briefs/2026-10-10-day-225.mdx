# Day 225 brief — Washington buys Russian diesel to hold its own fuel price as the IRGC claims a tanker on the bypass route

*Saturday, October 10, 2026 · Day 225 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Two things happened that the file has been waiting on from opposite directions. After a ninety-minute call with Putin, Trump announced that Russia will supply over 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 in November and a million thereafter, and the Treasury issued a general licence permitting sales of Russian-origin diesel through 7 April 2027. US diesel stands at $6.28 a gallon against $3.68 a year ago. Zelenskyy called the move "an investment in war". Separately the IRGC Navy claimed a strike on an LPG carrier it named as NV Sunshine on an "unauthorized route south of the Strait of Hormuz", warned that vessels using unauthorised passage would be pursued "throughout the region", and had Brig. Emirates, Etihad, flydubai and Air Arabia joined the Riyadh cancellations, and Cathay Pacific's suspension now runs to 31 January 2027. The Saudi-led coalition claimed 136 targets destroyed in Yemen and Bab al-Mandeb "cleansed". Pezeshkian said Iran has revised rather than answered. Brent held at $104.43 while VLCC freight set records. Direction holds escalating; the thirty-day probability rises from 9 to 10.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The diesel price, not the deal, is now setting American policy on two adversaries at once.**

The president who deferred strikes on Iran until after 3 November has, five days later, licensed Russian diesel sales through April 2027 and announced tonnages from a Kremlin he had been sanctioning. The sequence matters more than either act. Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework the political-will clock has stopped being an input to the war and become its governor — the oil-reserve clock is empty, the SPR is exhausted, the G7's hundred million barrels is the only active instrument and Germany's fifteen million is its first identified national tranche, so the remaining supply lever was an adversary's refineries. Two further readings follow. First, the sanctions-exhaustion prior recorded on Day 224 now cuts both ways: Treasury claims to have neutralised most of Iran's shadow fleet while simultaneously licensing Russia's, which prices the instrument as transactional rather than principled and weakens the threat value of the unfired bank designation. Second, Trump welcomed Russian involvement in ending the Iran war in the same call, which adds Moscow to Doha as a channel — the narrow reason the thirty-day probability moves at all. The counter-reading is Zelenskyy's, and it is the strongest one available: a licence that funds Russian refining is a transfer to a belligerent in another war, and the published tonnages do not reconcile with each other. Watch whether any barrels actually move inside the licence window, and whether the exemption is read in Tehran as proof that economic pressure is negotiable.

**2. The bypass route was the system's slack, and the IRGC has now claimed it as a target.**

This file has carried one question about the strait since the IRGC adviser's threat against "illegal routes": whether it would be acted on. It has been, and claimed. Roughly forty per cent of regional barrels have been bypassing Hormuz, Standard Chartered measured only sixty per cent of Gulf crude still crossing against eighty-three pre-war, and that southern lane is precisely what has kept volumes at or above pre-war while the system lost resilience. Analytical judgment: an attack on the bypass converts the resilience-not-volume prior from an observation into a trajectory — the slack that absorbed the blockade is now itself contested, and the energy-infrastructure clock worsens without a single barrel of production being hit. Two disciplines apply. The attribution-split prior holds: Tehran named NV Sunshine, Vanguard named the Panama-flagged GEM NO.2, UKMTO named neither and reported crew status unknown, so the hull is recorded as disputed. And §3.5.3 governs Naqdi's "full control", which is a claim by a belligerent about a waterway CENTCOM says it does not control. The market's answer was almost nothing in crude — Brent moved 0.14% — and everything in freight, with VLCC earnings at $1.01 million a day and MEG-China at a record $28.28 a barrel. Price is migrating from the cargo to the voyage.

**3. Taiwan: no fresh reading, and the record freight print is the number that matters.**

There is no Taiwan-specific development today; the CPC file stands as recorded, with the Executive Yuan's NT$415 billion package — NT$180.94 billion of subsidy and NT$233.83 billion of capital injection — still unvoted and the early-2027 insolvency assessment unaddressed in public. The relevant movement is in the freight channel this brief flagged yesterday. Kpler's September prints are records: MEG-to-China VLCC at $28.28 a barrel, up $12.90 month on month, VLCC earnings averaging $1.01 million a day, and MEG-Japan clean product at $19.68 rising to $23 by month end. Trading Economics attributed Friday's steady Brent explicitly to the Russian licence while noting Hormuz disruption pushing freight to record highs. Analytical judgment: the crude-is-not-gas prior is reinforced again, and the solvency-not-price prior with it. A cargo cost that is flat and a voyage cost that is at a record is the worst combination for a buyer on long-dated delivered contracts, because the escalation lands in the term that cannot be re-routed away and does not show up in the Brent screen the legislature is watching. Watch for the legislative vote, and for any Taipei official who separates delivered LNG cost from crude price — the conflation is still the thing most likely to under-size the package.
