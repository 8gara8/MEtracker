# Day 251 brief — Democrats take both houses of Congress, Brent falls below $95, and Tehran's leader says concessions were a mistake

*Thursday, November 5, 2026 · Day 251 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Republicans lost both houses of Congress in Tuesday's midterms, and the war on Iran was part of the reason by the President's own account. AFP, via The Peninsula, reported Democrats seized the House and flipped Senate seats in at least six states, including Texas, Kansas and South Carolina; the Associated Press projected at least seven Senate flips. Donald Trump congratulated the Democrats and told Fox News that "a part of it was Iran." A war-powers resolution that failed 49 to 50 in September is now expected to pass once the new Congress sits on 3 January, though a veto would still need two-thirds to override. Markets read the vote as a push toward a deal: Trading Economics put Brent at about $94.89 on Wednesday, down 8.7% on the day, while hormuz.now counted three AIS-visible tanker crossings a day against 47.6 before the war. Tehran read it as vindication. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, in a written message, said those who thought concessions could persuade Washington were gravely mistaken and warned of a response on a much greater scale to any renewed attack; Speaker Qalibaf wrote that Americans said no to war. The Qatari track produced a claim but no text: anonymous sources told Al-Araby Al-Jadeed the United States approved a Qatari draft memorandum, while Qatar's Majed Al Ansari said no particular development could be discussed. At sea, a Pakistani navigational warning reports the UAE-owned tug Selat Storm sunk off Musandam. Israel's President Herzog accepted the certified election results and began consultations. Direction stays mixed; the probability rises one point to 10 on the political-will clock, not the draft.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The vote moves the political-will clock, and the lame duck runs to 3 January.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the political-will clock moved further in one night than in any cycle this file has recorded, and it moved against the war. Trump told Fox News that a part of the result was Iran; a White House official said gas prices would fall when the conflict is solved; the Associated Press, via Al-Monitor, ties the Democratic gains to affordability concerns from the war. At CFR, Elisa Ewers expects hearings "to expose the failed strategy," and Michael Horowitz lists Epic Fury among the 2027 oversight targets. The limits are structural. The new Congress sits on 3 January, a veto override needs two-thirds in each chamber, and CNBC's sources doubt war-powers votes would bind the administration. That leaves about eight weeks in which the outgoing Congress and the President alone decide, and the window cuts both ways. It strengthens the case for a deal before January, which is how the market read it. It also strengthens the case for finishing the military option while the window is open: CNBC quotes Lintzenich saying "We still have pending action in Iran," and the New York Times, per a search-result summary of The New Arab, reported CENTCOM ready to strike as early as Wednesday. Neither a strike nor a stand-down was located. The probability moves from 9 to 10 on this clock alone. The reported US approval of a Qatari draft does not move it: no text, no named signatory, no session the United States attends.

**2. Tehran answers the vote with a harder line, and the market answers with a 9% fall.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock improves on paper while the doctrine variable hardens. Mojtaba Khamenei's written message for 13 Aban is the most senior text of the cycle, and it argues against the logic of an interim deal: those who thought concessions could relieve pressure were gravely mistaken, and a change of party in Washington will not end hostility. Read beside Javan's report, via Iran International, that Tehran rejected Qatar's five-day confidence-building proposal, and Rezaei's seven conditions, the message narrows what Pezeshkian's delegated authorities can concede. It also fits the answer-by-omission prior: the principal did not mention negotiations at all. The market priced the opposite. Brent's fall to about $94.89, roughly $10 below Tuesday's $104.5 print and more than $20 below the near-$118 of late October, came while hormuz.now counted 21 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days and no gas carrier. That is the diplomacy-priced-not-supply prior at its widest gap yet. The attack series did not pause for the vote. Pakistan's NAVAREA IX warning, via Riviera, reports the Selat Storm sunk off Oman's northernmost tip with its crew status unconfirmed, the first sinking in this file located by a third party with coordinates. Iran's IMO delegation calling the Council's reporting biased and incomplete is a claim about the record, not a denial of the attacks.

**3. Taiwan: no fresh Taiwan-specific development; the price relief is real but unhedged.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development was located this cycle, and prior assessments are unchanged; the supplementary budget for Taipower and CPC remains unpassed as far as this file knows. Two inputs move. On price, Brent under $95, if it holds, cuts the fuel-cost overshoot on the state utilities' balance sheets sharply against the near-$118 carried at the end of October. It was priced on an election and an unpublished draft, and one strike in the lame-duck window could reverse it in a session. On LNG, hormuz.now counted no LNG or LPG carrier crossing in the last seven days and one LNG carrier in thirty, and relays a September report that QatarEnergy extended force majeure into early December. For an importer that relies on Qatari cargoes, volume, not price, is the binding constraint, and it has not eased. China's envoy Zhai Jun is due in Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia this month; a Beijing-brokered Hormuz arrangement would carry its own strategic cost for Taipei.
