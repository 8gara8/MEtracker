# Day 243 brief — Israel's ballot ends without a majority, a missile reaches Ras Tanura, and the IAEA puts 440 kg back before the Security Council

*Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · Day 243 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Israel's ballot produced no majority for anyone. Exit polls put Gadi Eisenkot's bloc at 53 seats against Netanyahu's 50 on Kan 11 and 52-50 on Channel 12, with Arab parties at 17; Channel 14 alone gave the right 60 against 43. Likud stayed the largest single list at 24 to 25 seats — 30 on Channel 14 — against Yashar's 21 to 23. Eisenkot declared victory and claimed first right to form a government. The Tehran corridor survived and acquired no date. The 30-day ceasefire probability eases 7 → 6.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The ballot removed a majority without removing a prime minister, and the count runs a week.**

Analytical judgment: three exit polls put the change bloc ahead and none put either bloc at 61. Likud is still the largest single list, the Arab parties hold 17 seats and both principals have ruled out relying on them, and the official count does not finish until 4 November with the president's mandate due by 11 November. That is a fortnight in which Israel has a caretaker government, an unresolved treason complaint filed by Lieberman against the prime minister, and a chief of staff whose dissent on operational necessity was never answered on its merits. The institution-against-principal prior does not resolve on an exit poll, and the denial-by-deputy prior still stands: the only categorical statement that Israel would not strike before the election came from the defence minister. What does resolve is the pollster-provenance prior. Direct Polls' 61-45 for Netanyahu, run for Channel 14 and i24 and the only series producing a majority, never appeared; Channel 14's own exit poll stopped at 60. The six mainstream polls' 52-50 the other way is the number the ballot came closest to. Under the multi-clock framework the political-will clock has moved in Jerusalem without yet changing who holds the decision, and the American president endorsed the candidate who did not win a majority — five days before the senators' deadline and six before the midterms his own strike planning is reportedly keyed to.

**2. A missile reached the terminal, and the only confirmation is one unnamed person speaking to Bloomberg.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure clock moved further on Tuesday than at any point in this phase. Ras Tanura is not a pumping station, a regional airport or a headquarters floor: it is a 550,000 barrel-a-day refinery attached to the loading facilities through which nearly all Saudi crude leaves the Gulf, and the bypass that is supposed to answer Hormuz runs to Yanbu, which Saree has also named. The Brent trigger this file carries requires a confirmed Houthi strike on an Aramco production or export facility, and it is not met. Saree claims it; Bloomberg's single unnamed source describes a contained fire near the facilities after an attack in the region; Aramco's press office and the Energy Ministry say nothing; the coalition's own statement addresses three ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province without addressing the terminal. The claim-is-not-confirmation prior holds and the strike is not adopted. But the disclosure-is-a-choice prior cuts the other way: this is the government that published and named its Dammam dead, and its silence on the most consequential target yet claimed is a decision rather than a capacity. Note what the Eastern Province interceptions mean geographically — that is where Ras Tanura and Abqaiq sit, and no interceptor inventory figure has ever been published for this theatre.

**3. Taiwan: no fresh development in Taipei, and a fourth figure for the flows Taipei depends on.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-relevant development was reported on 27 October; the NT$71.1 billion Taipower supplemental budget remains unpassed with the December tariff review ahead, and prior assessments stand unchanged. The movement was in the flows themselves. ANZ Research put Strait of Hormuz transits at about 60 percent of normal with Gulf crude exports down a fourth consecutive week — a fourth live figure beside Kpler, the FT and MUFG at or near pre-conflict levels and the president's more-than-before-the-war claim. Under the contested-count prior none of the four is differenced against the others, but the direction of the disagreement has changed: until now every contested reading sat at or above pre-war, and this one sits well below. Freight on Saudi Arabia-China routes ran about $33 a barrel with some Middle East charter and demurrage rates above $1 million a day, and Brent is up roughly 90 percent year to date. For an importer that buys 94 to 97 percent of its energy and takes most of its LNG from Qatar and the UAE, the freight line and the 60 percent reading matter more than the headline barrel. The solvency-not-price prior continues to operate on CPC's and Taipower's balance sheets rather than on Taiwanese bills, which defers the shock without reducing it.
