key_developments:
  - "Saudi Arabia confirmed the East-West pipeline is pumping 5.8 million barrels a day, answering Day 221's first open question. Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said the roughly 1,200-kilometre line from the eastern fields to the Red Sea terminal at Yanbu is moving 5.8 million barrels a day against about 2 million in August — its lowest month since January — out of a maximum capacity of 7 million, and that after the drone damage of early September, blamed on Iraqi militia, \"within five or six days, we began using the pipeline again after the major attack that struck it\". Yanbu crude loadings have restarted. Saudi Arabia typically routes about 4 million barrels a day through the line, roughly 4% of global supply. Day 220 recorded the Khurais pumping station struck with conflicting reports on whether flow stopped; the minister's figure settles it in favour of running, and does so from the government rather than from a tracker. No Aramco damage assessment for Khurais, Rabigh or Riyadh has been published (Al Jazeera, The National, Saudi Gazette, OilPrice, Egypt Oil & Gas)."
  - "Two further trackers put the Hormuz recovery between 76% and parity, and the Day 220 divergence resolved into a band. Kpler data cited in market briefing put average daily crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz at 10.3 million barrels over the seven days to Saturday, 76% of the pre-war baseline. Shell's chief executive put Middle East flows near 80% of pre-war levels. Both sit alongside Kpler's separate September count of 16.5 million barrels a day of regional exports at pre-war parity with 40% bypassing the strait, and against Al Jazeera's standing description of Hormuz as largely closed since late February. Spot Brent fell below $100 on Tuesday and was quoted at $101.15 on Wednesday, up 0.26%, with WTI at $89.97; Brent is up 4.10% on the month and 54.54% on the year, with the fourth-quarter forecast at $106.60 and the twelve-month at $121. Nine tanker incidents were recorded in the strait this month. UK diesel reached a record near £2 a litre. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran \"loaded zero crude oil onto tankers last month\" under Operation Economic Outcast. President Trump signed an executive order easing red-dyed diesel restrictions for farmers and truckers (Newsquawk, Trading Economics, CBS News)."
  - "The UN published the first verified toll of the Yemen escalation: 959 dead, more than 5,100 casualties, 184,000 displaced. The World Health Organization recorded 959 deaths and more than 5,100 casualties since 6 August. The International Organization for Migration put displacement above 184,000 since 1 August, including 21,800 in the week ending 3 October — a 26% rise on the previous week — and 6,474 on 4 October alone, with 45% of assessed households previously displaced. IOM Director General Amy Pope said \"more and more people are thrown into an appalling cycle of displacement, living in fear of their lives and with their futures uncertain\". Cholera cases tripled within weeks and measles is spreading; safe shelter is the most critical need, with families sleeping outdoors and women and girls reporting a shortage of appropriate latrines. Seventy-two UN personnel and five former UN staff remain detained; one World Food Programme staff member was released. At the Geneva briefing IOM Djibouti chief of mission Alexandre Coissac reported more than 3,700 refugee arrivals from Yemen in three weeks, 539 Ethiopian migrants returned since 10 September, and that \"between 20 and 30 September, more than 3,000 stranded migrants had been observed\" facing \"dehydration, exhaustion, food and water shortages\" (UN News, WHO EMRO, UN Geneva, TASS, DevelopmentAid)."
  - "The Houthis denied losing ground and the battlefield tolls diverged by a factor of three. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group retains \"full control over all their gains on the ground\", dismissed government reports as \"fabricated heroics\" and claimed more than 200 opposing fighters killed or wounded. Reuters sources cited by Al Jazeera said government forces \"supported by heavy Saudi air strikes, reached the outskirts of Mocha on Monday as Houthi fighters retreated towards the highlands\", and Yemen's Defence Ministry said the city had fallen; the government also claimed the al-Manzara, al-Ashari and Dhiban mountains in Haifan south of Taiz, Dhubab airport and positions at Bab al-Mandeb. Al Jazeera said it \"has not independently verified the full extent of the government's territorial gains\". Operation Dawn of Yemen was announced on 4 October, President Rashad al-Alimi saying it was launched \"to restore state institutions, liberate the capital, Sana'a\". Coalition spokesman Major General Turki al-Maliki said \"the Coalition continues to provide full operational support\", with 100 fighter jets and 324 targets destroyed at the opening and 1,576 Saudi airstrikes claimed since mid-July. The Taiz Axis claimed about 100 Houthi fighters killed and 150 wounded on 5 October; American coverage reported more than 270 combatants killed in 24 hours around Dhubab. FDD analyst Bridget Toomey noted that independent observers Mohammad al-Basha, Ali al-Sakani and Taha Saleh questioned the Mocha claims (Al Jazeera, CBS News, Long War Journal)."
  - "Washington acknowledged its role in the Yemen offensive for the first time. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saudi Arabia and Yemen had \"a right to defend themselves\", and the United States is providing intelligence support to the offensive. Day 221 carried the question of whether American support would be officially acknowledged as an open deadline; it has been, at the level of intelligence rather than strike. Separately Trump said he remains open to direct negotiations with Iran and expects the Saudi-Houthi confrontation to be resolved, claimed the United States had eliminated Iran's military capabilities and secured the Strait of Hormuz, and predicted the war would end soon with prices falling. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the president was \"well aware of the risks to energy flows\" before launching operations. The White House denied that Trump had suggested Iran should \"take out\" Los Angeles and San Diego, saying he meant those cities would have faced threats from a nuclear Iran. A 22-year-old Briton was arrested as the seventh suspect in the suspected RAF Fairford plot; the bombers have not returned (Gulf News, Newsquawk, CBS News)."
  - "Turkish and Pakistani force numbers were still not published 24 hours after the Mecca pact was activated. The joint statement from the emergency Strategic Political and Defence Committee meeting in Riyadh \"reaffirmed their unequivocal rejection of any attack or threat against the kingdom's security\", said the three would \"move immediately to the practical implementation of the collective defence commitments\" and would \"ensure their rapid deployment in the kingdom\". No troop numbers, named units, deployment timetable beyond \"immediate\" and \"rapid\", or named officials at the Riyadh meeting have been disclosed, and no Iranian or Houthi response to the activation has been recorded. The three signatories are NATO's second-largest military, the Islamic world's only nuclear-armed state and the Gulf's largest power. Indian reporting noted that Pakistani plans to deploy troops in Yemen under the pact have prompted domestic warnings of entry into another conflict (Euronews, Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye, ThePrint)."
  - "The Houthis hit three airports in one cycle and Saudi Arabia conceded injuries for the first time. Saree announced three operations on 5-6 October using \"a large number of ballistic and cruise missiles and drones\" against King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the Aramco refinery at Rabigh in the Mecca region, Abha airport, the Khamis Mushait base, the Akfa camp and sensitive sites in Najran and Jazan provinces, presenting them as a response to 60 Saudi airstrikes on Yemeni provinces in a day and 1,576 since the escalation began, and renewing the warning to all global airlines against using Saudi airspace except over Mecca and Medina. Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation said on Tuesday that the attacks on Jazan and Najran airports \"resulted in three minor injuries and material damage\" — the first Saudi confirmation of effect after a cycle of denials. Air traffic was halted at Riyadh airport after a ballistic missile struck it. Riyadh did not confirm the King Khalid International or Rabigh claims. No airline has been recorded suspending Saudi routes, and no regulator has issued guidance (Anadolu Agency, Al Jazeera, NBC News, Newsquawk)."
  - "Satellite imagery showed renewed activity at an underground nuclear site bombed in March, with no inspector able to check it. The Institute for Science and International Security, analysing Vantor Technologies imagery from September to early October, reported \"significant renewed activity\" at the Minzadehei underground facility near Tehran struck by Israel in March, describing vehicles parked outside the security perimeter and \"many passenger vehicles and trucks\" inside it, and assessed that \"this activity may be indicative of an effort by Iran to reestablish a nuclear weapons development capability\". IDF spokesman Brigadier General Effie Defrin said the facility had housed nuclear scientists who \"worked secretly\" on weapons capability. The IAEA still cannot verify roughly 440.9 kilograms of 60% material and the Panel of Experts mandate expired on 26 September, so the first new signal about Iran's programme since the snapback came from a private think tank's commercial imagery rather than from an inspection. Separately a US delegation is expected in Israel to review compliance with the Visa Waiver Program after the flydubai attack by copilot Hamam al-Hammami, amid reports that some 1,240 aircrew from states that do not recognise Israel entered the country over the past year; Transport Minister Miri Regev proposed an aviation visa requiring Population Authority approval 48 hours before departure and requested NIS 40 million. The IDF, Shin Bet and police chiefs met to keep the 27 October election \"orderly and safe\", and the National Security Council warned of heightened Iranian and Hamas attack risk around the 7 October anniversary. Islamic State's attack near Kirkuk is now reported to have injured four officers besides the one killed (Times of Israel, Ynet, Jerusalem Post)."
  - "Tehran answered with surprises, a security chief's admission and an execution ledger of 70. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf told the chamber that \"the US has become frustrated in the face of the Iranian nation's resistance\", that \"Iran will continue to confront its adversaries with new surprises\" and that \"the era of buying time and imposing unilateral demands has ended\", restating that the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until all seven conditions under the Islamabad memorandum are met, describing recent American messaging as \"cognitive warfare\" and placing Iran's approach on \"power and rationality\". National security adviser Mohsen Rezaei, reported by IRNA, described the country's circumstances as among the most difficult periods it has faced, with the rial sharply down and the blockade having largely prevented oil exports. Pezeshkian again rejected talks with \"an enemy that assassinates and sanctions\". Iran International reported that 70 people have been executed in political and security cases since the January protests — nearly two a week — with 194 on death row, 113 of those cases tied to the January protests and 10 to the wars, 13 women and at least seven juveniles among them, 18 of the executed from ethnic minorities, 29 of them under 30, and 32 executions carried out in Karaj and 12 in Isfahan. UN rapporteur Mai Sato cited \"serious concerns about the treatment of defendants and fair-trial standards in the Alikhani Square case\" and said \"Alireza Sepahi's mother was tortured in front of him\"; Shirin Ebadi said of one execution, \"he became a groom yesterday, and today they executed him\" (Tasnim News Agency, Business Standard, Political Wire, Iran International)."

# Day 222 brief — The bypass is confirmed at 5.8 million barrels, the UN puts the first number on Yemen's dead, and a bombed nuclear site stirs

*Wednesday, October 7, 2026 · Day 222 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

The bypass stopped being a claim. Saudi energy minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said the roughly 1,200-kilometre East-West pipeline is pumping 5.8 million barrels a day against about 2 million in August — its lowest month since January — out of a 7 million capacity, and that after the early-September drone damage, "within five or six days, we began using the pipeline again after the major attack that struck it" (Al Jazeera). Yanbu loadings have restarted. Two more measurements arrived beside Kpler's parity count: Hormuz crude flows averaged 10.3 million barrels a day in the seven days to Saturday, 76% of pre-war, and Shell's chief executive put regional flows near 80% (Newsquawk, CBS News). Brent fell below $100 on Tuesday and was $101.15 on Wednesday (Trading Economics). The humanitarian ledger moved the other way and for the first time carries a verified figure: WHO recorded 959 deaths and more than 5,100 casualties in Yemen since 6 August, IOM 184,000 displaced since 1 August, 21,800 in the week to 3 October, with cholera tripled and measles spreading (UN News). Saree said the Houthis keep "full control over all their gains", claimed three operations against King Khalid International, Abha, Rabigh, Khamis Mushait, Najran and Jazan, and Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority conceded "three minor injuries and material damage" (Anadolu Agency, Al Jazeera). Rubio said Riyadh and Sanaa's government had "a right to defend themselves" and confirmed American intelligence support (Gulf News). Turkish and Pakistani force numbers were still not published. Direction holds escalating; seven-day risk extreme; spillover critical; the thirty-day probability holds at 7.

## Strategic Implications

**1. A bypass that is struck and running is a different object from one that merely exists.**

Day 221 opened the bypass-as-target prior on the premise that the infrastructure defeating the blockade had itself become a target and the export recovery was therefore reversible. Twenty-four hours later the Saudi energy minister put the East-West line at 5.8 million barrels a day against 2 million in August and 7 million of capacity, with Yanbu loading again and a stated repair time of five or six days after the September damage. Two further measurements narrowed the dispute: Kpler at 10.3 million barrels a day through Hormuz, 76% of pre-war, and Shell's chief executive near 80%. Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework the energy-infrastructure clock improves to strained from critical, because resilience has now been measured rather than asserted — a line hit and restored inside a week is a weaker hostage than a line merely unhit. The prior does not retire; it narrows to a question of repair rate against strike rate, and today the repair rate won. The Day 220 divergence between a parity count and a "largely closed" strait resolves into a band of 76% to 100% rather than a contradiction, which is the first time this series can quote a range instead of ten incompatible figures. Watch whether Yanbu or Fujairah is struck directly rather than at a pumping station, and whether Aramco ever publishes the damage assessment it has withheld for four days.

**2. The first verified number is a humanitarian one, and it came from the agency with nothing to win.**

For three days this series adopted no casualty delta in Yemen because the belligerents' claims were unnumbered on one side and unanswered on the other. The ceiling broke from outside: WHO recorded 959 deaths and more than 5,100 casualties since 6 August, IOM 184,000 displaced with 21,800 in a single week and cholera cases tripled. The combatant claims remain three-way incompatible — about 100 Houthi dead claimed by the Taiz Axis, more than 270 combatants in 24 hours in American coverage, more than 200 government fighters claimed by Saree alongside a denial of any loss at all. Analytical judgment: the humanitarian-escalation clock is now the only clock in this war with an audited figure attached, and the unverified-claim-ceiling prior is answered on the civilian axis while remaining unanswered on the military one. The WHO series is recorded and not merged, because it is Yemen-only from 6 August and overlaps the carried cumulative by an unknown amount; the reconciliation is a new standing deadline rather than an arithmetic exercise. Against that, Rubio's acknowledgement of American intelligence support confirms the delegated-escalation prior at the level of official statement: the campaign producing these numbers is fought by partners with American assistance now on the record.

**3. Taiwan: the crude bypass works, which is precisely why the gas exposure is worse.**

No fresh Taiwan-relevant development today; prior assessments are unchanged. What today supplies is the number the crude-is-not-gas prior was missing. Saudi Arabia can move 5.8 million barrels a day around the Strait of Hormuz through a pipeline it repaired in under a week; Qatari LNG has no Yanbu and no Fujairah, so none of that resilience reaches Taipei. The price series proves the point: CPC raised LNG 9.68% this month to NT$24.9096 a cubic metre, having already raised it 53.92% from March to May and 25.42% across August and September, leaving gas prices roughly doubled since late February, with about 80% of imported LNG going to power generation. Taipower's accumulated losses stood at NT$371.2 billion at end-August against NT$89.5 billion at CPC, and the NT$71.1 billion subsidy and NT$233.8 billion CPC infusion remain before the legislature unvoted. Analytical judgment: the fiscal-buffer prior holds against a Q4 Brent forecast of $106.60 and a twelve-month $121. Watch whether any party in Taipei distinguishes crude recovery from gas recovery in public — the headline parity invites exactly the conflation that would under-size the package.
