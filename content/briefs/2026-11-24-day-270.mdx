# Day 270 brief — IRGC claims three more mined tankers under US escort as Washington reportedly delays strikes and Israel's security chiefs see no urgency

*Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · Day 270 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran's IRGC Navy claimed three more tankers mined in Hormuz, this time under US air escort, as reports from Washington and Israel pointed to a delay rather than an imminent strike. The IRGC Navy said Monday night that one tanker hit a mine near the strait's southern coast and two east of it; no vessel was named and no US comment was located. Iran's army claimed a US LUCAS drone downed. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei called reports of a 14-tanker blockade-running convoy "media speculation" but said Iran would use all available means against the blockade. Ynet, via Iran International, reports Washington holding off any strike for at least a week, with Trump inclined to wait for the new year; i24NEWS says CENTCOM wants to attack or stand down. In Israel, Haaretz reports Mossad researchers see no urgency even as chief Roman Gofman lobbies for strikes, Channel 13 quotes Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir saying there is "no urgent need," and Gadi Eisenkot says the threat is not immediate; Netanyahu accuses rivals of minimizing it. Qatar's prime minister met Marco Rubio and JD Vance in Washington and warned the region cannot absorb another round; Iran reportedly summoned Qatar's chargé d'affaires over a German-Qatari statement. Treasury sanctioned 22 entities and eight people in Iran's shadow banking network, citing $14.7 billion in flows. The Washington Post reports some Senate Republicans open to cutting war funding before the 11 December deadline. Brent fell about 3.5% to roughly $89, a three-month low; hormuz.now still counts 2.3 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis. No casualty series changes. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 5.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The decision clock slips: Washington pauses, Israel's security establishment pushes back.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock eased by days rather than moved. Ynet, via Iran International, reports Washington has decided to hold off any strike for at least a week, with Trump inclined to wait until after the new year if talks fail; his advisers are split between another offensive and leaning on the blockade. That is a press report, not an announcement, and a US official quoted by Al Arabiya said Washington could still strike "at any time." i24NEWS says CENTCOM is at maximum readiness and wants either to attack or to stand down. In Israel the case for a short war is losing its institutional backing. Haaretz, via the Times of Israel, reports Mossad researchers judged no immediate need for action while chief Roman Gofman lobbied CIA Director John Ratcliffe for joint strikes; Channel 13 quotes Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir saying there is "no urgent need" and that Israel could wait for Washington and a new government. Gadi Eisenkot, whose coalition deadline Gulf News gives as 6 December, says the real danger is the roughly 440 kg of enriched uranium. Netanyahu, as caretaker, says rivals minimize the threat for political reasons. The caretaker-risk and Israel's-independence priors hold, but today's evidence cuts toward restraint inside the Israeli system. The political-will clock also moved: the Washington Post reports some Senate Republicans open to cutting war funding before 11 December, and Hakeem Jeffries says Democrats will seek to end the war in January. The probability holds at 5.

**2. At sea and in the Gulf: more mine claims, more sanctions, more mediation.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure and negotiation-capacity clocks moved in opposite directions. The IRGC Navy now claims three more tankers mined, one near the strait's southern coast and two east of it, and says they were under US air escort; no hull is named and no CENTCOM response was found. Iran's army separately claims a US LUCAS one-way attack drone. The mines-as-enforcement and claim-inflation priors apply. Gulf News carries a maritime analyst's report of a US strike on a tanker near Chabahar, also unconfirmed, and the Joint Maritime Information Centre's count of seven tankers hit since 16 November, against Al Jazeera's earlier count of about 25 since 14 November. Baqaei dismissed the 14-tanker convoy as speculation while saying Iran may use any means against the blockade; the blockade-as-trigger prior is unresolved. Washington answered with money, not missiles: Treasury's Operation Economic Outcast sanctioned 22 entities and eight people, at least eight companies in Dubai, citing $14.7 billion in shadow-banking flows since January 2025, and Scott Bessent said Iran's economy is "underwater." The economic-coercion-substitutes prior strengthens. Mediation thickened: Qatar's prime minister met Rubio and Vance, Egypt's Badr Abdelatty says Cairo is working with Qatar, Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, and Iraq's Ali al-Zaidi urged peaceful means. Against that, Iran reportedly summoned Qatar's chargé over a German-Qatari statement, and Baqaei says the seven conditions stand. In Yemen, Houthi-run outlets report 10 killed by Saudi strikes; Riyadh has not commented.

**3. Taiwan: crude falls on the delay while LNG stays near zero.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 16 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days, 2.3 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and unchanged from yesterday, with one LNG carrier in seven days and two in 30. PortWatch tonnage stays at 0.8% of normal to 15 November and Ras Laffan port calls at 28%. The AIS floor now sits beside much higher estimates: Kpler, via Trading Economics, puts Hormuz flows at about 9.5 million barrels a day in the ten days to 19 November, down from 12.2 million in October, and Al Arabiya cites 108 million barrels in a week. The gap is unreconciled. Brent fell about 3.5% to roughly $89 on Trading Economics, a three-month low, and Gulf News puts it at $88.75 with WTI under $80; the drivers cited are more time for talks, Libya's Sharara restart and the IEA's surplus. The oil-priced-as-surplus versus gas-shut prior sharpens: crude eases while Qatar's LNG stays under force majeure to end-December. For Taipower and CPC, cheaper crude does not offset the LNG gap at the winter peak, and a strike horizon pushed into the new year moves the risk window into January cargo planning. The markers remain crossings against 2.3 a day, LNG transits, Qatar's force majeure and the unpassed supplementary budget.
