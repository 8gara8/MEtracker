# Day 267 brief — Iran moves to test the US blockade as Trump calls its terms unacceptable; IRGC names bases in Europe; coalition strikes Hodeidah

*Saturday, November 21, 2026 · Day 267 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Washington's decision on a wider war stayed open while Tehran moved toward the blockade line. Iran signalled it will try to run the US naval blockade: Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said "the blockade must be broken," lawmakers and prayer leaders spoke of tankers sailing from Kharg for China, and Guards-linked Fars threatened Gulf oil facilities if the ships are fired on. Al-Monitor, via Iran International, counts more than 14 Iranian tankers near Kharg, most with transponders off; no convoy is confirmed. Washington has widened the blockade to ship-to-ship transfers, and Treasury sanctioned four Iranian "oil trustees." Trump said Iran "wants to make a deal" but not one "acceptable to me," and again pointed to after 1 January. Araghchi said the two-day Hormuz plan remains on the table but the enriched uranium "will not leave the country"; Witkoff and Kushner met Qatari officials in Berlin without significant progress, per Axios. IDF spokesman Effie Defrin said public guidelines are unchanged. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said Iran could strike US bases in Europe and any host country, as the EU and UK demanded an end to Iranian "hybrid attacks on European soil." The Iranian Navy claims a second tanker hit a mine; UKMTO reports a tanker struck and burning north of Khasab, without casualties. The Saudi-led coalition struck Hodeidah port and Sanaa; the Houthis claim strikes on Jizan and the Shuqaiq power plant. Iraqi forces began deploying in Jurf al-Sakhr. hormuz.now counts 1.9 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis; Brent is about $92.5. No new casualties were adopted. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 6.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The blockade line becomes the trigger.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock now runs through the blockade as much as through the White House. Day 266 placed the decision in Washington; this cycle Tehran moved to force it. Araghchi told ILNA "the blockade must be broken," lawmaker Mohammad Bayat urged readied tankers and an ultimatum, and prayer leaders in Ardabil and Tehran spoke of cargoes sailing from Kharg for China. Guards-linked Fars threatened explosions at oil facilities in all six GCC states if the ships are fired on. The motive is fiscal: Kpler logged no Iranian loadings in September, the first such month since 1979, and its analyst Homayoun Falakshahi expects Chinese payments for the last cargoes to run out by mid-December, after which Iran could lose $3-6 billion a month. The counters are real. No official has confirmed a convoy, the tankers near Kharg mostly have transponders off, and CENTCOM has extended the blockade to ship-to-ship transfers with a warning that non-compliant ships could face force. If a convoy sails, Washington must either let the blockade erode or fire on Iranian hulls, the direct test of the bypass-as-target and infrastructure-reciprocity priors. Mohebbi's warning that host states and even US bases in Europe are targets, and Vahidi's talk of a changed defence doctrine, widen the retaliation map. Israel is ready but quiet: Defrin says guidelines are unchanged, as he did the day before 28 February. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 6.

**2. Diplomacy narrows to Doha and Islamabad as the war's edges spread.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock is alive but stalled. Araghchi says the two-day plan stands, with Washington meeting its commitments on day one and Iran reopening the strait on day two, and that messages pass only through Qatar and Pakistan. He also drew a red line: the 60% stockpile "will not leave the country," though a "fair formula" is possible. Trump's answer is that Iran "wants to make a deal" but not one "acceptable to me." Witkoff and Kushner met Qatari officials in Berlin on Thursday without significant progress, per Axios via Iran International, and Treasury's four "oil trustees" designations fit a State Department signal of more economic pressure. Qatar's reported window closes around 24 November, three days away. The political-will clock frames the choice: funding lapses on 11 December, Democrats are split over cutting war money, and the bipartisan defence bill carries only $69 billion of Trump's $350 billion request. Europe is now a party in its own right. The EU-UK summit demanded Iran end "hybrid attacks on European soil" and signed a "No Tolls/No Fees" pledge against the toll-institutionalization risk, while Merz, beside Qatar's emir, called for a permanently open strait. The second chokepoint widened: the coalition struck Hodeidah port and Sanaa, and the Houthis claim strikes on Jizan and Shuqaiq. Iraq is the counter-trend, with forces deploying in Jurf al-Sakhr and Baghdad calling the change permanent.

**3. Taiwan: oil looks loose, gas stays shut, and a convoy fight would sit beside the LNG lanes.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 13 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days, 1.9 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and up from 1.6 yesterday, with still no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days (one of each in 30). PortWatch puts Ras Laffan port calls at 28% of normal and tanker tonnage at 0.8% to 15 November. Brent rose about 0.6% to roughly $92.5 but is on course for a third weekly fall, as Trading Economics cites abundant supply, the IEA's 2 mb/d fourth-quarter surplus and a Goldman oversupply warning: the oil-priced-as-surplus prior holds while gas remains the binding constraint. Two risks sharpen. First, Fars named UAE oil and Qatar among targets if Iranian tankers are fired on; Ras Laffan and Das Island are the plants that matter for Asian LNG buyers. Second, Iran's own winter gas deficit is projected to reach 430 million cubic metres a day with mazut and LPG exports halted, raising Tehran's incentive to break the blockade before the heating peak. For Taipower and CPC, the relevant markers remain QatarEnergy's force majeure to end-December and the unpassed supplementary budget.
