# Day 264 brief — US jets strike Qeshm after the third Bahrain launch as Kuwait reports drones; Qatar meets Iran with no outcome published

*Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · Day 264 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

The United States answered Iran's Bahrain launches for the first time since Trump's "unless provoked" line, and each side framed its fire as a response to the other. Hours after IRGC drones were fired at a US radar at Isa Air Base on Tuesday, US fighter jets struck what a US military official called an IRGC missile-launch site on Qeshm Island with precision munitions, "in response to IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets." Iranian media said the target was an air-defence launcher in the Nariman area, and Hormozgan's deputy governor said no residential, commercial or vital infrastructure was damaged; no casualties were reported on either side. IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi, quoted by RT, warned that any renewed aggression "no matter how minor" would meet "devastating and painful strikes." Kuwait's military said its air defences engaged hostile drones on Tuesday night without naming their origin. The GCC secretary-general asked the UN Security Council to act, Saudi Arabia called for an immediate halt to Iranian attacks, and Qatar condemned them while holding its scheduled meeting with Iranian officials; no outcome was published. The ECO communique in Islamabad backed Qatari and Pakistani mediation but did not mention the two-day Hormuz offer that Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said ECO states supported. At sea, UKMTO reported a tanker crew unaccounted for after an attack north of Khasab, while Iran claims more mine strikes. In Israel, the Knesset elected Yashar's Matan Kahana speaker by 66-52, strengthening Gadi Eisenkot's bloc. hormuz.now counts 2.0 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis; Brent stood near $93.3. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases to 8.

## Strategic Implications

**1. A first answer on Qeshm, sized to match the provocation.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock moved from a test to an exchange. For the first two launches at Bahrain Washington issued only interception notices; after the third, it struck. A US military official called the Qeshm raid self-defence against "IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets," tying it to the Bahrain launches without a White House statement or a named trigger. The symmetry is the point. Iran's drones did no damage by CENTCOM's account; the US strike did none to infrastructure by the Hormozgan deputy governor's account, and Iranian media placed it on an air-defence launcher, not the missile site the US named. Both sides can claim a response while neither has a casualty to avenge. That keeps the calibrated-host-strike prior alive and turns restraint-as-posture into proportional reply: "unless provoked" now appears to mean small strikes for small launches. The risks sit at the edges. Vahidi's warning, as RT reports it, promises "devastating" strikes for any aggression "no matter how minor," a line absent from Press TV's own account of his speech; which audience gets which version is worth watching. Kuwait's night-time air-defence activity, origin unstated, would extend the rhythm to a second host if it proves Iranian or militia fire. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases from 9 to 8 because a strike-and-counter-strike loop has opened, even at low intensity.

**2. Mediation widens on paper while the substance stays where it was.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock shows more endorsements and no movement. Qatar held its meeting with Iranian officials on Tuesday; spokesman Majed Al Ansari described periodic delegation meetings meant to narrow gaps between proposals and said no party benefits from "neither war nor peace." No outcome, text or US interlocutor was published, and the reported window closes around 24 November, six days away. The ECO communique is the useful divergence. Araghchi said ECO states responded positively to Iran's offer to reopen Hormuz within two days if Washington releases assets, lifts sanctions and ends the blockade; the communique, as The Peninsula reports it, backs Qatari and Pakistani mediation in general terms and does not mention Iran or Hormuz. Araghchi's account therefore stays a claim. Gulf positions hardened in parallel: the GCC secretary-general asks the Security Council to act, Saudi Arabia calls for an immediate halt while urging diplomacy, and Qatar condemns the attacks while mediating, the mediator-pleads prior in its plainest form. China's 17 November briefing did not mention Iran, leaving the Shenzhen trilateral, expected on Thursday with the White House forecasting no deal, as the next test of whether Beijing or Moscow will carry anything. In Israel, Kahana's election with Arab-party votes strengthens Eisenkot's bloc but leaves Netanyahu in office during coalition talks, so the caretaker-risk prior holds.

**3. Taiwan: no direct development; a strike inside the strait barely moves oil while gas stays shut.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 14 tanker crossings in seven days, 2.0 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis, up from 1.9 but within noise, with no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days and one of each in 30. Kpler's preliminary estimate stays near 8.3 mb/d, about 48% of the 17.1 mb/d pre-war level. Brent stood near $93.3 on Tuesday, up 0.7% after Monday's 3.6% fall, with Trading Economics citing the IEA's surplus forecast of about 2 mb/d and a Goldman warning of a glut next year. The energy clock stays split for Taiwan: a US strike inside the strait moved oil by less than a percent, while gas through Hormuz stays at zero and, on this file's record, QatarEnergy's force majeure runs to end-December for some buyers. Cheap crude does not refill an LNG gap. Two risks grew at the margin. A tanker crew is unaccounted for north of Khasab, the first such report in this file's recent cycles, which bears on war-risk premiums for substitute cargoes. Kuwait's drone alert puts a second northern Gulf exporter in the line of fire. The Shenzhen trilateral is the next East Asian read. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
