# Day 269 brief — Iran claims two more mined tankers as Trump sets a new-year horizon for strikes; IDF intelligence warns Netanyahu of a multifront war

*Monday, November 23, 2026 · Day 269 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran claimed two more mined tankers in Hormuz and a 20th US MQ-1 downed, while Donald Trump set the new year as his horizon for renewed strikes. Iran's navy said two tankers that entered a prohibited zone east of the strait with transponders off caught fire after hitting mines; the IRGC Navy warned shipowners that following CENTCOM guidance risks "bankruptcy." UKMTO, via Shafaq News, reported only an undetailed tanker-military encounter in the Gulf of Oman; no vessel has been named. Bahrain's interior ministry said Saturday's Iranian attacks injured a Bahraini citizen and a Pakistani resident, the latter critically, after earlier reporting no injuries from the industrial-site strike; the GCC and Arab League condemned the attacks, and Saudi Arabia warned Iran would bear the consequences. An unnamed US official told CBS the attacks caused no damage to US forces. Trump told Fox he is inclined to resume strikes after the new year without a deal, called Iran's negotiators "professional tap dancers," and said blockade runners would end up "at the bottom of the sea." Channel 12 reports IDF Military Intelligence warned Netanyahu that a strike could open several fronts, against his and the Mossad chief's three-to-five-day expectation. Masoud Pezeshkian and Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf both said the "neither war nor peace" state cannot continue; Kazem Gharibabadi said the diplomatic window is still open. Bloomberg reports London preparing for renewed US strikes. The Houthis claimed a strike on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery, unconfirmed by Riyadh. hormuz.now still counts 2.3 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis. Two Bahraini-reported wounded are added. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases to 5.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The new-year clock: Washington names a horizon, Israel's own intelligence warns against a short war.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock acquired a date. Trump told Fox he is inclined to resume strikes after the new year if no deal is reached, called Iran's negotiators "professional tap dancers," and said tankers testing the blockade would be "at the bottom of the sea." On Truth Social he said Iran had launched more drones and missiles in the past week than at any time in over a year, all knocked down, and tied the escalation to the Democrats' midterm win. Both are claims; the second is not matched by any published CENTCOM tally. In Israel, Channel 12 reports that Military Intelligence warned Netanyahu at a closed meeting last week that a strike could ignite Lebanon, Syria and Gaza, against his and Mossad chief Roman Gofman's expectation of a three-to-five-day operation. Kan says Netanyahu is pressing Trump to strike "as soon as possible," while White House officials who favour more time for sanctions gain influence. The caretaker-risk and Israel's-independence priors sharpen: Avigdor Liberman, chairing the temporary Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee, fears unilateral action. Tehran answered on two registers. Pezeshkian and Ghalibaf said the "neither war nor peace" state cannot continue, Gharibabadi said the window is still open, and Mohsen Rezaei mocked Trump's "bigger than Yalta" post. Both capitals now reject the limbo, for opposite reasons. Bloomberg reports London preparing for renewed strikes and weighing limits on US use of British bases. The probability eases to 5.

**2. Mines, hosts and the Gulf: Iran raises the cost, the Arab states close ranks.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure and coalition-cohesion clocks moved together. Iran's navy and the IRGC Navy claim two more tankers mined east of Hormuz and near Ghanam Island, saying they ran dark through a prohibited zone; the IRGC told shipowners that following CENTCOM guidance risks "bankruptcy." UKMTO's only published item, via Shafaq, is an undetailed tanker-military encounter in the Gulf of Oman, so the mines-as-enforcement prior again outruns confirmed hulls. The IRGC also claims a 20th MQ-1, its fourth US drone in 24 hours; no US comment was located, and the claim-inflation prior applies. Al Jazeera's Tehran correspondent counts about 25 tankers hit since 14 November. On land, Bahrain now reports two injured, one critically, from Saturday's attacks, and its foreign ministry rejected the IRGC's account of hitting Patriot radars. The GCC, Arab League, UAE, Qatar and Saudi Arabia condemned the attacks; Riyadh warned of consequences. Oman's ambassador in Tehran, by contrast, pledged to keep working with Iran to restore normal shipping. Rami Khouri reads the campaign as an effort to show Gulf hosts that Washington cannot protect them; Trita Parsi warns that continuing the blockade means escalation; Hassan Ahmadian calls it a binary choice forced on Trump. The second chokepoint stays hot: the Houthis claim Rabigh and an Al-Dawasir radar, the coalition reports drones downed, and Taiz officials count 80 civilians killed by Houthi fire from 1 to 20 November. Qatar's reported window closes around 24 November with no readout.

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 16 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days, 2.3 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and unchanged from yesterday, with one LNG carrier in seven days and two in 30. Brent is harder to read: Trading Economics's table shows about $92.3, while its summary text cites $90.46; either way the oil-priced-as-surplus prior holds as the IEA projects a 2 mb/d surplus. For Asian LNG buyers the changed variable is timing. A named new-year horizon for strikes overlaps QatarEnergy's force majeure to end-December and the Northern Hemisphere winter peak, and a renewed campaign would land in the window in which Taipower and CPC must secure replacement cargoes. The markers remain crossings against 2.3 a day, LNG transits, Qatar's force majeure and the unpassed supplementary budget.
