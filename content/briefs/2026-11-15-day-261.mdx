# Day 261 brief — Trump signals he may hold off on Iran unless provoked as mine claims multiply; UN counts 25 seafarers dead while Iran demands the blockade end

*Sunday, November 15, 2026 · Day 261 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Washington signalled restraint while Iran's mine campaign continued. Reuters, via the Times of Israel, reports that President Trump told senior advisers he may hold off on new strikes unless Iran or the Houthis provoke the United States, after Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman urged him on 31 October to let sanctions work. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said further military action is not needed. Against that, Reuters says a third aircraft carrier is heading to the region, and Trump told Hugh Hewitt it would be "a defeat" if Iran's government survives his term. At sea, Iran's navy said a tanker caught fire after hitting a mine east of the strait on Saturday morning, and the IRGC Navy later said two tankers exploded on the southern route; counts differ across statements, no hull is named and Reuters could not verify. hormuz.now counted 1.9 tanker crossings a day, down from 2.3, against 47.6 before the crisis. At the Security Council, UN Assistant Secretary-General Khaled Khiari said at least 25 seafarers have been killed and that the situation "cannot be allowed to become the new normal." Iran's deputy envoy Gholamhossein Darzi demanded an end to the blockade and guarantees, and claimed more than 5,000 Iranian dead, well above the 3,559 official toll. Iran's Vienna mission said normal inspections "cannot and will not resume." In Israel, Netanyahu said the Iranian threat must be removed "once and for all" while Gadi Eisenkot rejected his unity offer. The White House says Trump will raise Iran with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen next week. The Saudi-led coalition downed two Houthi drones. Direction eases to mixed; the probability holds at 10.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Restraint as a posture: Trump may hold off, but a third carrier and a regime-change yardstick point the other way.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the political-will clock is now doing more work than the active-deadline clock. Reuters, via the Times of Israel, reports that Trump told advisers he may hold off on attacking Iran if there are no new provocations from Tehran or the Houthis, after Mohammed bin Salman urged him on 31 October to let sanctions work and Erdogan pressed for restraint in three calls. Rubio told the Federalist Society that further military action is not needed and that the measure of victory is an Iran without a nuclear weapon. Three things cut against reading this as de-escalation. First, the condition is broad: a mined tanker or a Houthi drone on Riyadh could count as provocation, and both were claimed this cycle. Second, the capability signals run the other way: a third carrier is moving in and all three could stay, even as Reuters reports some munitions stockpiles at worrying levels. Third, Trump told Hugh Hewitt that the regime surviving his term would be "a defeat," a goal no blockade alone delivers. The official-claim rule applies: "may hold off" is a sourced account of private remarks, and a White House official said only Trump knows what he will do. The domestic clock is clearer: a Reuters/Ipsos poll cited by Iran International has 71% wanting Congress to end the war, Thomas Massie says he can force a war-powers vote by mid-December, and funding expires on 11 December. Restraint is a posture, revocable on the next incident. Direction eases to mixed; the probability stays at 10.

**2. Iran argues to the Council, not to Washington, as mine claims multiply.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock is unchanged, but each side is now arguing to third parties rather than to each other. At the Security Council, Iran's deputy envoy Gholamhossein Darzi set three conditions for talks, an end to aggression, the lifting of the blockade and credible guarantees, and said host states owe Iran "full reparation." He claimed more than 5,000 Iranians killed, including 254 children; that is Iran's diplomatic figure, well above the 3,559 official toll and HRANA's 3,636, and this brief holds it apart. Araghchi's Kyodo op-ed frames reparations as a legal obligation, not "political goodwill." Iran's Vienna mission said normal safeguards "cannot and will not resume under abnormal circumstances," answering the IAEA's diversion finding with a refusal. The one opening is in the Times of Israel's account: officials say Iran will not ship out its highly enriched uranium but will discuss down-blending it under UN supervision. That is a sequencing offer, not a concession on the stock. At sea the claim pattern repeats. Iran's navy reported one tanker on fire; the IRGC Navy reported two; CBS logged a three-tanker statement; no hull is named, and Reuters could not verify. Khiari said 25 seafarers have died and the IMO has logged 136 incidents. In Israel, Netanyahu said the threat must be removed "once and for all," while Eisenkot rejected his unity offer; Likud and Yashar meet on Sunday. The caretaker-risk prior holds.

**3. Taiwan: crossings fall again, Qatar's force majeure runs to year-end, and Iran is on the Shenzhen agenda.**

Analytical judgment: two indirect Taiwan-relevant developments, both touching the energy-infrastructure clock. hormuz.now counted 13 tanker crossings in the seven days to 14 November, 1.9 a day against 47.6 before the crisis and down from 2.3, with no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days and one LNG carrier in 30. Kpler data on the same tracker put oil flow near 8.3 mb/d, about 48% of pre-war, consistent with CENTCOM's claim that 1.7 billion barrels have moved under US protection: crude moves under escort or by pipeline; gas does not. The tracker lists Qatar's LNG force majeure as extended to the end of December for some buyers, later than this file's early-December assumption. For Taipower the binding variable is now a year-end Qatari gap in peak winter demand, with war-risk cover at 7–9% of hull value against 0.25% pre-war. The second item is diplomatic: the White House says Trump will raise Iran with Xi and Putin at APEC in Shenzhen next week, though a US official told Reuters, "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement." For Taipei that is a variable to watch, not to price; Beijing's foreign ministry has said nothing on Iran since 13 November. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
