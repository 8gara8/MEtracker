# Day 272 brief — Trump says the war is funded through January as Washington weighs leadership strikes, Iran claims more mined tankers and a Houthi attack wounds 11 in Riyadh

*Thursday, November 26, 2026 · Day 272 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Signals from Washington point to a narrowing window rather than a decision. President Trump told the New York Post the war is "funded" until about the end of January, suggesting a window before Democrats take Congress, and said US goals differ from Israel's because he cares only about the nuclear program. NBC, via Fox News and the Times of Israel, reports renewed internal discussion of targeted strikes on Iran's leaders. Israel's caretaker defence minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons and that joint plans with CENTCOM "are ready"; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the claim a "confession" meant to drag the US into war. At sea, the IRGC Navy claims two more tankers mined early Wednesday on an unauthorised route and Iran's army claims a LUCAS and an MQ-9 drone; no hull is named and no US response was located, while UKMTO reports a projectile exploding near a tanker 50 nautical miles west of Dubai without damage. Army chief Amir Hatami says Hormuz will be "increasingly closed" until Iran's seven conditions are met. The Saudi-Houthi war widened: Saudi civil defence says 11 people were injured in an attack on a Riyadh financial-district tower, Saudi air defences downed four drones over Ras Tanura, and the coalition reports a projectile at Jazan after striking Hodeidah. The US embassy in Baghdad warned Iran is trying to target US officials. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says Iran has run through its floating oil stocks. Brent rebounded to about $92-94 from a three-month low; hormuz.now counts 2.4 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis. No casualty series changes. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 5.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The window narrows: a January funding horizon, leadership-strike talk and a chemical-weapons claim.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline and political-will clocks have converged on January. Trump told the New York Post the war is "funded" to about the end of January, tying his new-year strike horizon to the seating of a Democratic Congress on 3 January; that narrows the window rather than setting a date. NBC's report, carried by Fox News and the Times of Israel, that officials have renewed discussion of targeted strikes on Iran's leaders adds an option short of a full campaign; a White House official said only that the blockade continues "with full effect." These are reports and remarks, not decisions. In Israel, caretaker defence minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons, has not reactivated its nuclear programme though "there are signs it is trying to," and that joint IDF-CENTCOM plans "are ready," with Trump to decide. Araghchi called the claim a "confession" meant to drag Washington into another war; Avigdor Liberman said it does not justify independent Israeli action. The security-establishment-restraint prior holds, but the public case for action is being rebuilt around a new weapons claim, and the claim-inflation prior applies on both sides. Trump's statement that his only goal is the nuclear file, unlike Israel's, strengthens the Israel's-independence prior. Neither side moved toward talks: Hatami and Ghalibaf restate the seven conditions. The probability holds at 5.

**2. At sea and in the Gulf: unconfirmed mines, a wider Saudi-Houthi war and embassy alerts.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure clock worsened at the edges while the core picture held. The IRGC Navy claims two more tankers hit mines early Wednesday after taking a "fabricated route," and Iran's army claims a LUCAS and an MQ-9 drone over the strait; none is confirmed, and JMIC, via Iran International, calls Monday's three-tanker claim unconfirmed. UKMTO's report of a projectile exploding near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai, without damage, is the only independent incident located. Iran is formalising control: Hatami says the strait will become "increasingly closed," army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia says ships must coordinate with the armed forces, and the Persian Gulf Strait Authority blacklisted 26 tugboats. The mines-as-enforcement and toll-institutionalization priors strengthen. The second-chokepoint prior sharpened most: Saudi civil defence, via SPA, says 11 people were injured in Tuesday's Houthi attack on a Riyadh financial-district tower; Saudi air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura, which the Houthis claim set the refinery alight; and the coalition reports a projectile at Jazan and a drone near Jeddah after its strikes on Hodeidah. The US embassy in Baghdad warned that Iran is trying to target US officials and could hit US interests anywhere. Mediation continues without traction: Qatar's prime minister met Egypt's Badr Abdelatty in Cairo, and China's ambassador urged a return to talks.

**3. Taiwan: crude rebounds on Gulf attacks while LNG stays near zero.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. Brent recovered from Tuesday's three-month low below $90: Trading Economics quotes $94.29, up 3.6%, though its statistics panel shows $92.44, citing the Ras Tanura drones and Iran's mine claims, tempered by recovering Gulf exports, an IEA surplus and rising US stocks before OPEC+ meets on 29 November. hormuz.now counts 17 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days, 2.4 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis, with two LNG carriers in seven days and Ras Laffan port calls at 29% of normal. Kpler, via Iran International, says Gulf exports excluding Iran ran just under 14 million barrels a day in September and October, near prewar, with November preliminarily about 12 million; hormuz.now carries a Kpler estimate of 8.3 million b/d for the week to 11 November. The AIS floor and the Kpler figures remain unreconciled. Iran's own crude exports have effectively stopped, and Kpler warns its revenue could dry up by mid-December. The oil-priced-as-surplus versus gas-shut prior holds: crude flows around Iran while Qatar's LNG stays under force majeure to end-December. For Taipower and CPC the exposure is the winter-peak LNG gap, not crude, and a strike window framed as running to end-January overlaps February cargo planning. Markers: crossings against 2.4 a day, LNG transits, OPEC+ on 29 November and the unpassed supplementary budget.
