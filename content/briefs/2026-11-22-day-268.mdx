# Day 268 brief — Iran strikes US positions in Bahrain after escorted tankers are hit off Khasab; Qatari team in Tehran; Houthis fire at Riyadh

*Sunday, November 22, 2026 · Day 268 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran answered US escort flights over Hormuz with repeated strikes on Bahrain, while a Qatari delegation held talks in Tehran. The IRGC said it fired missiles and drones at US positions at Al-Riffa and Awali and hit two Patriot radars, after earlier claiming strikes on Sheikh Isa air base and Juffair. Bahrain's interior ministry said an Iranian strike set a civilian industrial facility on fire, with material damage and no injuries; the GCC and Jordan condemned it. Press TV quoted CENTCOM as saying attacking drones were shot down without damage or casualties. At sea, UKMTO reported two tankers struck by projectiles off Khasab, without casualties; the IRGC said US aircraft had covered "unauthorized" transits and claimed two mine explosions and a 58th MQ-9 downed. CENTCOM rejected an Iranian Army claim that it damaged a US F-16, saying all US fighter jets are "safe and accounted for." Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the Qatari team held two hours of talks and called the blockade an act of war; Abbas Araghchi, at Oman's National Day, said the strait stays shut until Washington accepts Iran's conditions, while mediation "must be given a chance." No tanker convoy has sailed, and TankerTrackers now calls the idea "wishful thinking." Saudi Arabia intercepted three Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh and one toward the western region, and issued a danger alert for the Mecca region. hormuz.now counts 2.3 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and one LNG carrier in seven days; Brent is about $92.6. No new casualties were adopted. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 6.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Escort against blockade: the war at sea runs both ways.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock moved from talk of an Iranian convoy to a fight over US-escorted traffic. The IRGC says F-16s, F/A-18s, F-35s, P-8s, helicopters and drones flew cover on Friday night for "unauthorized" tankers; it claims two mine explosions, off Khasab and near Ghanam Island, and a 58th MQ-9 downed. UKMTO describes a different mechanism: two tankers leaving the Gulf were hit by projectiles 11 and 17 nautical miles from Khasab, without casualties. Its weekly count shows 263 transits to 18 November, about 65% below pre-war, and at least 19 strikes on vessels this month, the war's highest monthly total. The IRGC then carried the fight ashore, firing at Al-Riffa and Awali in reply to MQ-1 and MQ-9 flights and claiming two Patriot radars. Bahrain reports a fire at a civilian industrial site and no injuries; Press TV quotes CENTCOM saying drones were downed without damage. CENTCOM's flat denial of the F-16 claim fits the claim-inflation prior, but the pattern matters more than any one claim: each escorted transit now draws an Iranian strike on a host state, the strike-the-hosts and proportional-reply priors in operation. The convoy has not sailed. Windward counts 31 laden sanctioned tankers near the blockade line, but TankerTrackers retracted its early read. Andreas Krieg's view that Iran cannot break the blockade militarily, only make it costly to enforce, fits the evidence. Direction stays escalating; the probability holds at 6.

**2. Qatar in Tehran, Oman at the podium, Washington content to wait.**

Analytical judgment: the negotiation-capacity clock flickered without moving. Gharibabadi says a Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on Friday evening; Araghchi called such visits routine, then used Oman's National Day to say the strait stays shut until Washington "acknowledges its mistakes" and meets earlier commitments, while asking that regional mediation be given time. Tehran's conditions remain maximal: an end to war on all fronts, unfrozen assets, lifted oil sanctions and an end to the blockade, which Gharibabadi called an act of war. The uranium red line holds. Washington signals patience. Trump told Fox he wants a permanent deal and said Saudi Arabia asked him to let the situation continue for now; Bessent calls Iran's rulers "wounded cornered animals" and says Iran has no oil on the water. The FT, via Press TV, reports CENTCOM warning that holding the posture past year-end degrades readiness, so the interceptor and political-will clocks press against that patience. Qatar's reported window closes around 24 November, two days away. Israel was publicly quiet this cycle; the caretaker-risk prior rests on earlier reporting of Netanyahu's push and the Mossad chief's lobbying. The second chokepoint widened again: the Houthis fired three missiles at Riyadh and one toward the west coast, and Saudi Arabia alerted the Mecca region. Lebanon's Aoun told Iran to stop interfering and Israel to withdraw "from every inch," the Lebanon gap restated by Beirut itself.

**3. Taiwan: crossings tick up under escort, but the escort is now the target.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold. hormuz.now counts 16 AIS-visible tanker crossings in seven days, 2.3 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis and up from 1.9, with one LNG carrier in seven days and two in 30. That is a floor, since AIS misses dark ships. PortWatch still puts tanker tonnage at 0.8% of normal to 15 November and Ras Laffan port calls at 28%. Brent was about $92.57 on 20 November, set for a third weekly fall as Trading Economics cites abundant supply, record Hormuz flows claimed by US Energy Secretary Chris Wright and the IEA's 2 mb/d surplus: the oil-priced-as-surplus prior holds. The risk for Asian LNG buyers is that the ships being hit are the escorted ones. If US cover draws mines, projectiles and strikes on Bahrain, every escorted transit is priced as a combat event, and the few LNG cargoes moving stay hostage to that cycle. Fars's target list still includes Qatar. For Taipower and CPC, the markers remain QatarEnergy's force majeure to end-December, crossings against the 2.3-a-day baseline and the unpassed supplementary budget.
