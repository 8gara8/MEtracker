# Day 258 brief — Ghalibaf puts the ball in Washington's court as Rezaei hardens terms; tanker attacks reach UAE waters and Eisenkot warns Netanyahu off Iran moves

*Thursday, November 12, 2026 · Day 258 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Tehran says the next move is Washington's as the sea war reaches UAE waters. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's lead negotiator, told Al Jazeera that Iran sent its response through a Qatari mediator, that "the ball is now in the American court," and that the seven conditions must be met before talks begin. He said the Strait of Hormuz will "never return" to its pre-war status under an Iran-Oman understanding. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei told Al Mayadeen, per Tasnim, that "even the smallest amount" of enriched material will not leave Iran and that ending the war in Lebanon is a fundamental condition. Iran International, citing the Wall Street Journal, reported that Trump privately questions the June memorandum and that JD Vance is weighing direct talks with Rezaei. The IRGC Navy, via Tasnim, said two more tankers struck mines and were left burning. UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile off Oman, crew safe, and ADNOC L&S said one of its vessels was damaged in an attack in UAE waters. Rezaei threatened US bases "in European countries," and deputy armed forces chief Keyoumars Heydari said Iran's response has "no geographical limitations." In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot urged caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to refrain from "dangerous moves that can wait," amid Channel 12 reports of a renewed dispute with the army chief over Iran; the Prime Minister's Office called the letter "an irresponsible move." The Houthis claimed strikes on Riyadh's airport and a Yanbu pumping station; the coalition said it intercepted three cruise missiles. Brent rose to about $92.5 while hormuz.now counted 2.4 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 before the war. Direction stays mixed; the probability holds at 13.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Ghalibaf says the ball is in Washington's court; Washington argues over the last deal.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the negotiation-capacity clock now runs on a reply rather than a meeting. Ghalibaf's account to Al Jazeera is the first Iranian confirmation this cycle that a response travelled through Qatar, which partly corroborates Al Hashmi's progress claim, but it confirms process, not terms. He said Iran's demands, including lifting the blockade and sanctions, releasing frozen funds and halting fighting on all fronts, must be implemented before negotiations begin. Rezaei added that no enriched material will leave Iran and that Lebanon is a fundamental condition, which forecloses the dilution idea aired on state television and restates the sequencing wall and the Lebanon gap in their hardest form. The Wall Street Journal report relayed by Iran International cuts both ways: Trump reportedly sees the June memorandum as a costly mistake, yet Vance is said to be weighing direct talks with Rezaei. Both rest on unnamed officials, and no US statement answered Ghalibaf. His claim that US officials asked Putin to mediate is single-source and would revive the Moscow-as-venue prior if confirmed. The G7 backs regional mediators but rejects any Hormuz toll, which collides with Ghalibaf's "never return." The probability holds at 13: a pending reply is movement, but each side's precondition is the other's endpoint, and Qatar's reported window to about 24 November has 12 days left.

**2. The sea war reaches UAE waters as Tehran widens its target set and Israel's transition becomes a security question.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure clock worsened. Three accounts cover 10-11 November. The IRGC Navy and the regular Navy, via Tasnim, claim more tankers hit mines in a restricted zone east of the strait; UKMTO reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile about 19 nautical miles northeast of Limah, Oman; and ADNOC L&S said a vessel was damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters. gCaptain says it is unclear whether these overlap. Tehran says mines, UKMTO says a projectile, and no hull is named, so the hull-named-by-the-third-party prior holds. An attack inside UAE waters, if Iranian, would end the 64-day run without a confirmed Iranian attack on a host state's territory; it is unattributed, so the count continues with a flag. Chatham House argued on 9 November that Iran may reignite the conflict to break the blockade; this cycle's attacks fit that reading without proving it. The declared-scope prior widened: Rezaei named US bases in Europe, Heydari described an expanded target list with "no geographical limitations," and Treasury confirmed Bessent moved to a military base over Iranian threats. The Israel's-independence prior took a new form. Eisenkot's letter, sent amid Channel 12 reports that army chief Eyal Zamir again opposed action Netanyahu wanted against Iran, makes the caretaker period itself a risk vector; the PMO says every decision was coordinated with the security establishment. Spillover stays proxy-led: the Houthis claim Riyadh's airport and a Yanbu pumping station, against a coalition interception claim.

**3. Taiwan: no fresh Taiwan-specific development; crossings slip to 2.4 a day as Brent bounces to $92.5.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development was located, and prior assessments are unchanged. Price followed the sea war rather than the talks this cycle: Trading Economics shows Brent at about $92.5, up roughly 1.2% on Wednesday, bouncing from a 10-week low and trimming an intraday high near $94.6, citing Iranian denials that negotiations are under way. Volume fell again. hormuz.now counted 17 tanker crossings in seven days to 11 November, 2.4 a day against 47.6 before the crisis and down from 2.6, with no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days and one in 30; PortWatch still puts tanker tonnage at 0.1% of normal. For Taipower's gas-heavy fleet the binding variable remains Qatari LNG, and two facts bear on it. An attack in UAE territorial waters, if confirmed, means the Gulf side of the strait is no longer a safe staging area, raising insurance costs for the first cargoes after any deal; gCaptain counted about 162 ships at anchor off Fujairah and Khor Fakkan as of 8 November. And Ghalibaf's "never return," with an Iran-Oman oversight understanding, points to a permanent permit regime the G7 rejects; under the toll-institutionalization prior, even a deal could leave LNG transits priced and conditional. Qatar's Al Misnad told the Paris Peace Forum the disruption reaches fuel, fertilisers and household budgets. Late November, when the reported window and QatarEnergy's force majeure converge, remains the date to watch. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
