# Day 255 brief — Trump calls new war 'highly unlikely' as Araghchi threatens 'hell' and mediators stop travelling; a Houthi attack damages an Aramco tower in Riyadh

*Monday, November 9, 2026 · Day 255 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Trump and Tehran traded conditional threats while the mediation itself stalled. Trump told CNN that renewed war is "highly unlikely" if Iran signs "a very strong agreement," but said three carriers are "locked and loaded" and that US forces could destroy 38 Iranian bridges in about an hour, per Al Jazeera and Rudaw. Abbas Araghchi answered on X that Iran would "rain down hell" if attacks resume and that its two-day proposal "stands," while saying mediators have stopped travelling since the US midterms: "We have to wait." A Ynet headline quotes Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying the US is "not close to breakthrough with Iran" and that its demands are "ridiculous and unrealistic." IRNA reported four explosions on Qeshm, which no US source confirmed, and the IRGC Navy said two more unnamed tankers caught fire after hitting mines. CBS reports a separate tanker fire near Khasab and a UAE rescue of 22 crew from a burning cargo ship in the Sea of Oman. Iran's military called CENTCOM's new Task Force Iron Gate "wordplay," and Tasnim says Iran's security council has ratified the Rasht-Astara railway deal with Russia, the kind of land route the task force targets. Spillover widened: a Houthi attack damaged an Aramco tower in Riyadh's financial district, which Saudi Arabia's energy ministry called an attempted attack with minor damage, and a projectile halted the Khor Mor gas field in Iraqi Kurdistan, which the regional electricity ministry says cuts 2,800 megawatts. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot received the mandate and invited Netanyahu to join him. hormuz.now counted 3.1 crossings a day against 47.6 before the war. Direction stays mixed; the probability holds at 10.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Conditional threats on both sides, and a mediation that has stopped moving.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the negotiation-capacity clock slowed even as both principals talked about a deal. Araghchi's own account is the clearest evidence: mediators have not travelled since the US midterms, and Tehran says "we have to wait." His two-day proposal is now framed around "requirements, including end to war on all fronts," which reaches Lebanon and Yemen and restates the sequencing-wall prior in wider form. Trump's line on CNN, that renewed war is "highly unlikely" if Iran signs "a very strong agreement," is conditional optimism from one party. The counter comes from inside his own administration, in a Ynet headline quoting Rubio that the US is "not close to breakthrough with Iran" and that Tehran's demands are "ridiculous and unrealistic." The coercive half of Trump's message was specific: three carriers "locked and loaded," "the other one just arrived," and 38 bridges that could go "in literally one hour," while Israel's Channel 13, via Rudaw, says CENTCOM told Israel that strikes on Tehran would target power plants and bridges. That points the active-deadline clock at infrastructure rather than air defences if the lame-duck window is used. Tehran's rhetoric hardened in step: Rezaei told Washington "you lost the war and the election," and IRGC spokesman Mohebbi called the conflict an "existential war." No session, venue or text is named. The probability holds at 10.

**2. The blockade's land turn meets a Russian railway, and the Gulf periphery takes the hits.**

Analytical judgment: the coercion and spillover clocks moved at the edges rather than in the strait itself. Tehran called Task Force Iron Gate "merely a wordplay," per CBS, yet the same day Tasnim reported that the Supreme National Security Council had ratified the Rasht-Astara railway agreement with Russia, with the last 37 km of the 162 km route handed to the Russian contractor. That is the North-South corridor where a land-and-Caspian blockade would have to bite, so the blockade-goes-inland prior now has a named counter-move, though the line is far from carrying freight. Trump says Iron Gate is "in full force and effect"; no action by it has been reported. At sea, the hull-named-by-the-third-party prior is partly tested: the IRGC Navy again names no ship for its two mined tankers, but CBS reports a UKMTO tanker fire near Khasab and a UAE rescue of 22 crew from a burning cargo ship in the same window. Neither report names a vessel or links it to a mine. The periphery took the heavier blows. A Houthi attack damaged an Aramco tower in Riyadh's financial district; Saudi Arabia's energy ministry calls it an attempted attack with minor shrapnel damage, while witnesses cited by Tasnim describe extensive damage across several floors. In Iraqi Kurdistan a projectile halted Khor Mor, which supplies most of the region's power, and Telegram channels tied to pro-Iran Iraqi groups claimed a suicide drone. Iran itself is not shown to have struck a host state, so the 61-day count holds.
