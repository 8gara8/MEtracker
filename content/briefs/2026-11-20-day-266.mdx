# Day 266 brief — Trump weighs a short war as Netanyahu calls it unavoidable; Iran rejects 'neither war nor peace'; Iraq defuses Jurf al-Sakhr

*Friday, November 20, 2026 · Day 266 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

The strike loop quieted while the decision over a wider war moved to Washington. Axios and the New York Times report that Trump ordered the Pentagon and intelligence agencies to assess Iran's likely responses to Central Command attack scenarios, as Netanyahu presses for a four- to seven-day joint air campaign; Channel 13 says he told ministers renewed war is "unavoidable," and an IDF officer says plans are "shared and approved." ## Strategic Implications

**1. Washington weighs a short war while Iran rejects "neither war nor peace."**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock has shifted from the host-state loop to a decision in the White House. Day 265's reading was a cycle of small US strikes answered through Kuwait and Bahrain. This cycle's sources located no new host-state launch, but the strategic question widened. Axios reports Trump tasked the Pentagon and intelligence agencies with modelling Iran's responses to CENTCOM attack scenarios and asked whether any option could avoid large-scale attacks on Gulf energy; the New York Times says Netanyahu wants a four- to seven-day campaign, with targets that per Axios include Pickaxe Mountain, bridges, power plants and leadership. These are anonymous-source claims, and the counters are concrete. Trump says he is "not rushing" and points to "after the beginning of the year"; US officials told Channel 12 the Israeli push looks politically motivated; IDF chief Eyal Zamir is reported as the lone senior opponent of immediate strikes; and the USS George Washington has left, leaving two carriers. Tehran's signalling hardens in parallel. The foreign ministry warns of a "decisive response" to any recurrence, and IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebi says Iran rejects "neither war nor peace," claims three new air-defence systems and promises a response "three times" larger, possibly before an enemy act. The IRGC Navy's claim of two mined supertankers is unverified, but JMIC rates the strait threat severe. Direction stays escalating; the probability eases from 7 to 6.

**2. Congress, Baghdad and Shenzhen: three off-ramps, one in use.**

Analytical judgment: the political-will and negotiation-capacity clocks moved in opposite directions. The Senate defeated a war powers resolution 51-48, with Collins, Murkowski and Paul in favour and Fetterman against; a Reuters/Ipsos poll puts approval of the war at 31%. The constraint arrives on 11 December, when funding lapses and Representatives Ro Khanna and Yassamin Ansari say they will oppose any bill that does not bar offensive operations, and on 3 January, when Democrats take both chambers. The lame-duck-window prior strengthens: Israel's caretaker government and a president with six weeks of unified control share an incentive to act early. Baghdad offers the one de-escalation. Senior Shiite leaders brokered a deal under which government forces enter Jurf al-Sakhr on Friday and Kataib Hezbollah relocates; Shafaq reports units already moving and a unified command ordered, while Al Arabiya, via Iran International, says the withdrawal is phased over a month. Esmail Qaani's role suggests Tehran chose to protect its ally by conceding ground. Shenzhen produced nothing on Iran in public: China's 19 November briefing did not mention it, Putin spoke of shared responsibility for security, and Trump said he needs no help. At the IAEA, Iran, China and Russia tied verification to an end to "unprovoked aggression," while the US and European three accused Iran of stonewalling. Qatar's reported window closes around 24 November, four days away, with no named US negotiator.

**3. Taiwan: the IEA names the shock as gas stays at zero.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold, but the IEA's verdict raises their weight. Fatih Birol's World Energy Outlook calls the Hormuz crisis the "largest threat to energy security in history," puts cumulative Gulf losses at 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of gas, and sees no "credible pathway" back to the pre-war system. hormuz.now counts 11 tanker crossings in seven days, 1.6 a day against 47.6 pre-crisis, and no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days (one of each in 30). PortWatch puts tanker tonnage at 0.8% of normal to 15 November, while Kpler, via Gulf News, puts crude through the strait at about 8.5 mb/d, half of pre-war. Brent rose 0.76% to about $92.43 on Thursday as strike reporting added a risk premium, but it remains near its lowest since late August, with the IEA projecting a 2 mb/d surplus this quarter: the oil-priced-as-surplus prior holds while gas stays shut. Two marginal risks grew. Axios reports the White House asked whether any strike option could avoid large Iranian attacks on Gulf energy, an acknowledgement that the infrastructure-reciprocity risk reaches Qatar's export base. And a TankerTrackers-reported Iranian crude loading tests the blockade; an interdiction would add a flashpoint near the lanes substitute cargoes use. QatarEnergy's force majeure runs to end-December on this file's record.
