# Day 256 brief — Qatar's prime minister takes the Iran file to Trump, who calls the war 'effectively over'; Tehran insists on its seven conditions and a drone darkens Iraqi Kurdistan

*Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · Day 256 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Mediation moved back to Washington. Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, met Vice President JD Vance and then Donald Trump at the White House on Monday, and said on X that they discussed "ways to progress the US-Iran talks," per The Peninsula and Iran International. Trump then spoke of "positive momentum," called renewed strikes "quite unlikely" and the war "effectively over," per Al Jazeera, while saying Gulf states favour keeping the naval blockade and that Iran accepts a nuclear-weapons ban "100%." Tehran did not move. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei wrote that it was "time to SURRENDER to the seven conditions," a senior official told Tasnim no Rezaei-Vance meeting in Doha is planned, and spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the two-day proposal "the most logical roadmap." President Masoud Pezeshkian said the Leader has ordered an end to "neither war nor peace" and that Iran will negotiate "with dignity" but "not surrender to force." Tasnim reported that "US projectiles" struck Qeshm on Sunday night, and a Hormozgan deputy governor said they caused no damage; no US statement was located. Baghaei also said Iran cannot meet IAEA obligations "like it did under normal conditions." Spillover continued: a drone halted the Khor Mor gas field, cutting up to 2,800 megawatts and leaving more than 70% of Iraqi Kurdistan without power, and Saudi Arabia said its East-West pipeline was hit near Medina with operations unaffected. China's foreign ministry said prolonging the US-Iran conflict "does not serve the common interest." Brent fell about 6% to near $92; hormuz.now counted 2.9 crossings a day against 47.6 before the war. Direction stays mixed; the probability rises to 12.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Qatar carries the file to the White House, and Tehran answers with its seven conditions.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the negotiation-capacity clock restarted at the mediator level a day after Araghchi said mediators had stopped travelling. Sheikh Mohammed's trip is the first mediator journey this file records since Tehran's "we have to wait": about 45 minutes with Trump after a separate session with Vance, who Iran International says has spoken with him almost daily for two weeks. That partly tests the mediation-pause prior. It is not retired, because no Iranian interlocutor was in the room and no session with Tehran is named. Trump's readout is optimism from one party and is graded as such: "positive momentum," "effectively over," and an Iranian acceptance of a nuclear-weapons ban "100%, more than 100%" that Tehran publicly denies. The skeptical counter came the same day. Rezaei's "time to SURRENDER to the seven conditions," the Tasnim denial of any Doha meeting with Vance, and Baghaei's demand that Washington lift the blockade and unfreeze assets before Hormuz reopens all restate the sequencing wall: conditions first in Tehran, nuclear first in Washington. Pezeshkian's line that the Leader has ordered an end to "neither war nor peace" cuts both ways. It reads as a mandate to close a deal or as a warning that the limbo will end by other means, and he paired it with "we will not surrender to force and bullying." The probability rises two points to 12 on the restarted channel, not on any change in terms.

**2. Qeshm acknowledged, IAEA access narrowed, and the hawks' clock in Washington.**

Analytical judgment: the active-deadline clock is unchanged in grade but sharper in evidence. Tasnim now reports that "US projectiles" struck Qeshm on Sunday night, and Hormozgan deputy governor Ahmad Nafisi said the targets lay outside residential and service areas, with no damage. That replaces Fars's Day 255 warning-shot explanation and fits the defensive-scoping prior: limited US fire, minimised by Tehran, with no CENTCOM statement located. Al Jazeera relays US reports that Hegseth and CENTCOM commander Brad Cooper are pressing Trump to resume attacks and, citing Axios, that CENTCOM awaits orders for major operations; a Ynet headline says Netanyahu calls Trump "every day." The political-will clock runs the other way: House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said Iran is "stronger now" and vowed to end the war "one way or the other," and Thomas Massie reintroduced impeachment articles against Hegseth. On the nuclear file, Baghaei said Iran cannot fulfil IAEA obligations "like it did under normal conditions," while saying it has not suspended the NPT and that Bushehr was inspected days ago. With 440.9 kg of 60% uranium unverified, Washington's nuclear-first demand meets a narrower inspection offer. Spillover stayed on the periphery: Khor Mor, now unclaimed per Al Jazeera, and Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, the main Hormuz bypass, hit near Medina. Iran itself is not shown to have struck a host state; the count reaches 62 days.

**3. Taiwan: no fresh Taiwan-specific development; Brent falls on diplomacy while LNG transits stay at zero.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development was located, and prior assessments are unchanged. The diplomacy-priced-not-supply prior got its sharpest test yet. Brent fell about 6% on Monday, to $92.16 on Trading Economics' table and $91.61 on Iran International's account, the lowest since 31 August, on the Qatar meeting and Trump's "effectively over." The physical strait moved the other way: hormuz.now counted 20 tanker crossings in seven days, 2.9 a day against 47.6 before the crisis, down from 3.1, with no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days and one of each in thirty; IMF PortWatch deadweight was 0.6% of normal to 1 November. Trading Economics again says Gulf crude exports are recovering, which the strait counts do not show unless bypass routes carry the difference; the East-West pipeline that would carry it was itself hit on Monday, with operations reported unaffected. For Taipower's gas-heavy generation, crude relief does little: the binding variables remain Qatari LNG and zero transits, with QatarEnergy's force majeure last reported into early December. A lower Brent eases CPC's crude import bill and the inflation outlook, but not the LNG shortfall. The energy-infrastructure clock also moved in Iraqi Kurdistan, where Khor Mor's loss cut up to 2,800 megawatts, a domestic grid shock rather than an export one. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
