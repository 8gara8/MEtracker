## Executive Summary

Iran's supreme leader spoke for himself and widened the target lists. A Geneva firm placed Russian military satellites over 40 Saudi target areas, and a Saudi deputy minister confirmed the intelligence flows on the record. ## Strategic Implications

**1. The principal spoke, the answer arrived, and the two point in opposite directions.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework the negotiation-capacity clock stays strained and the active-deadline clock worsens, both on the same two statements. For seven cycles this file has asked whether Mojtaba Khamenei would appear and whether Rezaei's answer existed. Both were answered in one day, and the answers disagree. The supreme leader, still unseen, ordered target lists updated to cover all American and Israeli assets and promised a response with no geographic limits. Rezaei said the American tone had completely changed, called a third American reply much better and said it showed Washington prepared to accept Iran's seven conditions — then said the strait stays closed and there will be no negotiations until those conditions are met. Both cannot be the same signal. The disciplined reading is that the deal track gained a claimed concession relayed solely by the Iranian principal, with no American confirmation and no text, while the strike track gained a targeting instruction from the man who decides. The doctrine debate is the sharpest new fact: Rezaei says Iran is weighing whether to keep its longstanding ban if fighting resumes, and claims no decision. Grossi's unverified material and the absence of observed reconstruction both still hold, and no American official has answered him. The probability holds at 9 rather than moving, because only a text, a named signatory or a convened session the United States attends moves it further.

**2. The strait named its hulls and the Pentagon named its dead, and two long absences closed in one cycle.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure clock worsens while the political-will clock finally registers a disclosure. Two of this file's oldest demands were met. After six renderings of the hull claim with no vessel named, the strait produced the Louise 1 and the RITI — one struck by two limpet mines attached by a small boat off Khasab, the other burning with 25 crew taken off by Oman's navy. Limpet mines are a new weapon class here and require a boat alongside rather than a drifting field: a different capability and a different attribution problem. And after thirty-one cycles of non-disclosure, the Pentagon's own Defense Casualty Analysis System raised the American toll to 23 dead and 865 wounded, superseding 17/440 and substantially vindicating the senators' 23/861 — a reconciliation that arrived through a database rather than from any named official, three days before the deadline the senators set. The disclosure-is-a-choice prior is amended rather than retired: the department answered the arithmetic while leaving Warren's letter, the base-damage estimate and the $47bn unaddressed. The bounded-denial prior governs the strait again. CENTCOM denied the halting claim, denied any Guard presence and called the four-tanker claim false, all scoped to the primary routes while the Iranian claims concern the southern corridor Iran itself declared off-limits. The statements remain compatible, and the denial retires nothing.
