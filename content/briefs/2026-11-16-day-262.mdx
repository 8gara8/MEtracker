# Day 262 brief — Iran fires at a US radar in Bahrain again as Yemen's government quits Bab el-Mandeb; Araghchi heads to Islamabad and Muscat

*Monday, November 16, 2026 · Day 262 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran tested Washington's "unless provoked" line within a day of it being reported. The IRGC said six drones targeted a US air-defence radar at Riffa air base in Bahrain early on Sunday, calling it the second phase of retaliation for US "aggression"; Bahrain's Defence Force said it intercepted "several" Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets, and no damage or casualties were reported. It was the second launch at Bahrain in three days, so the host-state count resets. Qatar, Jordan and Egypt condemned the strikes, and Bahrain asked the Security Council to act. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called them self-defence under Article 51 and said Iran "will not leave any military attack or aggression unanswered." No US military response was located by this morning. In Yemen, government forces withdrew from the Bab el-Mandeb area to defend Aden; Reuters puts the Houthis about 80 km from the city and AFP about 60 km. The Saudi-led coalition reported intercepting two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and drones over the Eastern region, Makkah and Madinah. At sea, the IRGC Navy said two tankers running dark exploded on mines; no hull is named, and hormuz.now still counts 1.9 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 before the crisis. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran faces "one of the fastest collapses in history"; Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf called the remarks vulgar and said Iran will not stay in a state of "neither war nor peace." Mossad chief Roman Gofman is back from Washington to brief Israel's security cabinet. On diplomacy, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is in Islamabad and goes to Muscat on Tuesday. Direction returns to escalating; the probability eases to 9.

## Strategic Implications

**1. The provocation test: Iran fires at Bahrain again a day after Trump's reported restraint.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the active-deadline clock has become a conditional one, and Iran has just pressed on the condition. Reuters reported on Day 261 that Trump may hold off on new strikes absent provocation from Iran or the Houthis. Within a day the IRGC said six drones targeted a US air-defence radar at Riffa in Bahrain, its "second phase" of retaliation, after a Friday strike that CENTCOM said caused no damage or injuries. Bahrain says it intercepted the attacks; nobody reports damage. Al Jazeera's Resul Serdar reads two launches at Bahrain in three days as a possible new rule of engagement, and Baghaei calls it Article 51 self-defence. The calibrated-host-strike prior now describes a pattern, not an incident. Whether Washington treats intercepted drones at a Bahraini base as the provocation Trump described is the question for the next 72 hours; no CENTCOM statement on Sunday's launch was located. Israel is pushing the same way. Mossad chief Roman Gofman returned from talks with CIA Director John Ratcliffe to brief a security cabinet that, per Ynet via Iran International, could consider authorising an operation. US intelligence, per the Wall Street Journal via the Times of Israel, puts Iran's ballistic missiles at 1,500–2,500, a "low-confidence" range, and a Western official told Bloomberg production may reach 50 a month: the interceptor clock runs against the defenders. Direction returns to escalating; the probability eases from 10 to 9.

**2. Yemen's war reaches the second strait as Saudi cities come under fire.**

Analytical judgment: the energy-infrastructure clock now has a second chokepoint in play. Yemeni government forces announced a "redeployment" from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area to defend Aden, ending, per Reuters, a six-week counteroffensive to retake the Red Sea coast the Houthis seized in September. Houthi sources told AFP they hold more than 150 km of coast from Dhubab to near Khor Amira; Reuters places their forward units about 80 km from Aden and AFP about 60 km. Holding the shore is not closing the strait, and no new attack on shipping there was reported this cycle, but the proxy-periphery prior has become territorial. The pressure on Saudi Arabia is direct. The coalition reported two ballistic missiles intercepted over Riyadh and drones over the Eastern region, the Makkah region and Madinah; the Houthis claimed hits on King Khalid airport, a financial centre and two oil pumping stations, none verified. Saudi authorities count at least 26 killed since 23 September. With Hormuz shut, Saudi crude leans on the East-West pipeline to Yanbu; a Houthi coast at the southern strait puts that bypass's exit route under threat, the bypass-as-target prior in its sharpest form. The humanitarian clock worsens as well: Taiz remains besieged, and displacement estimates run from 300,000 (Reuters) to more than 340,000 since August (IOM, via Al Jazeera). The Houthis' release of 600 prisoners with ICRC support is a small counter-signal.

**3. Taiwan: no direct development; the gas gap holds and a second chokepoint comes into view.**

Analytical judgment: no fresh Taiwan-specific development today; prior assessments hold, with one indirect addition. hormuz.now counted 13 tanker crossings in the seven days to 15 November, 1.9 a day against 47.6 before the crisis and flat on the previous cycle, with no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days and one LNG carrier in 30. Kpler puts oil flow near 8.3 mb/d, about 48% of pre-war: crude moves under escort or by pipeline; gas does not. QatarEnergy's force majeure runs to the end of December for some buyers, and war-risk cover costs 7–9% of hull value against 0.25% pre-war. The addition is Bab el-Mandeb. Qatari LNG to Taiwan does not use the Red Sea, but some of the substitute cargoes Asian buyers chase from the Mediterranean would, and so does Saudi crude leaving Yanbu. A Houthi-held coast at the southern strait would squeeze the substitutes rather than the original supply. Brent last settled at $95.84 on 13 November; Trading Economics showed conflicting weekend indications near $94–95, so Monday's settle is the first real read after the Bahrain strikes. Beijing's foreign ministry has not briefed since 13 November, and the Shenzhen trilateral, with Iran on the agenda, is days away. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
