# Day 260 brief — Iran fires drones at Bahrain for the first time since September as mines hit more tankers; Mossad chief in Washington while negotiators fear Netanyahu

*Saturday, November 14, 2026 · Day 260 of the US-Israel war on Iran*

## Executive Summary

Iran fired at a Gulf host state for the first time in about two months. The IRGC said its Aerospace Force "targeted and destroyed" a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, in retaliation for an air attack on a telecommunications tower in Sirik. US Central Command said the IRGC launched two one-way attack drones toward Bahrain and US forces shot both down, with "no injuries or damage." Reuters, via Al Jazeera, calls it the first claimed Iranian attack on US military assets in a Gulf country since early September. No one has claimed the Sirik strike, and no Bahraini statement was located. At sea, Iran's army said two more tankers hit mines and caught fire in the eastern strait, and the IRGC Navy said a gas carrier hit a mine on Friday; none was named. hormuz.now counted 2.3 tanker crossings a day, down from 2.9. IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi promised "devastating and painful strikes" for the slightest aggression. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei warned that states joining US operations would be treated as participants, a message Korean media read as aimed at Seoul's naval expansion south of Oman. A confidential IAEA report said the agency cannot conclude that no nuclear material has been diverted. In Israel, Mossad chief Roman Gofman met CIA Director John Ratcliffe in Washington, and Channel 12 reported that army chief Eyal Zamir is the only security chief against an immediate strike. The Times of Israel reports that US, Iranian and Qatari negotiators fear Netanyahu could torpedo talks but still think a deal is possible. Qatar and Türkiye signed a defence pact. The IEA says supply now exceeds demand; Brent settled at $95.84. Direction turns escalating; the probability holds at 10.

## Strategic Implications

**1. Iran fires at a host state again, in a form built to be intercepted.**

Analytical judgment: under the multi-clock framework, the interceptor clock moved for the first time in weeks, and the host-state count ends at 65 days. The IRGC claimed it "targeted and destroyed" a US early-warning radar in Bahrain; CENTCOM said both drones were shot down with "no injuries or damage." The divergence is the information. Two drones are a signal, not a strike package, and the IRGC framed the launch as retaliation for an air attack on a telecommunications tower in Sirik that nobody has claimed. The official-claim rule cuts both ways: the radar's destruction rests on the IRGC alone, the absence of damage on CENTCOM alone, and Al Jazeera's Washington correspondent says neither can be confirmed. What is not disputed is that the IRGC chose a Gulf host and a US asset, the first such claim since early September, per Reuters. That gives substance to Rezaei's "no safe haven" and to Vahidi's promise of "devastating and painful strikes." No Bahraini statement, US retaliation or Trump comment was located by this morning, consistent with Washington's reported preference for the blockade and sanctions over renewed combat. The defensive-scoping prior holds: CENTCOM stressed interception, not response. The risk is ladder logic. The IRGC has now publicly committed to answer any repeat "without limits," which shortens the step from signalling to an exchange neither negotiating team wants. Direction moves to escalating; the probability holds at 10 because no channel closed.

**2. Israel's spy chief goes to Washington while negotiators fear Netanyahu.**

Analytical judgment: the caretaker-risk prior, eased yesterday, tightens again, and the active-deadline clock now turns partly on Israel's security cabinet. Axios, via the Times of Israel, reports that Mossad director Roman Gofman met CIA Director John Ratcliffe in Washington on Friday about Iran and may see envoy Steve Witkoff in Miami; Channel 12 says Netanyahu did not tell Eisenkot at Thursday's briefing. Channel 12 lists Netanyahu, Gofman, Shin Bet's David Zini, the National Security Council's Shmuel Ben Ezra and Ambassador Yechiel Leiter as favouring a strike, with chief of staff Eyal Zamir the lone holdout. These are sourced claims, not decisions. Or Asher of the Tachlith Institute told Al Jazeera that a caretaker prime minister "can legally take Israel to war." Against that, the Times of Israel's Jacob Magid reports that US, Iranian and Qatari negotiators all fear Netanyahu could torpedo the talks, yet still believe a final or interim deal is possible. The gaps are the standing ones: Iran will not ship out its enriched uranium, wants reparations, and wants tolls including on military vessels. The IAEA's confidential report strengthens Israel's case: the agency "is not able to conclude that there has been no diversion," does not know where the Isfahan enrichment plant is, and calls the gap a "serious proliferation concern." Iran replies that inspections cannot resume under US threats. Israel's independence prior is live again: pressure on the 24 November window may come from Jerusalem, not Washington.

**3. Taiwan: Seoul becomes the test case for East Asian navies near Hormuz; the oil surplus does not reach LNG.**

Analytical judgment: one indirect Taiwan-relevant development. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei warned, via Tasnim, that any state deploying forces or logistics in support of US operations commits "a hostile and unlawful act." Korea JoongAng Daily reads it as aimed at Seoul, whose cabinet approved moving the Cheonghae unit south of Oman with unmanned minesweeping equipment, pending a National Assembly vote; Seoul cites "noninvolvement and nonintervention." For Taipei this is a template: an East Asian importer that sends escort or mine-countermeasure assets toward Hormuz should expect to be named by Tehran. On supply, the IEA says global output, 105.4 mb/d in October, exceeds demand for the first time since the war began, with Gulf exports 26% below pre-war; Brent settled at $95.84, down about 2.5% on the week, per CNBC. The recovery is crude, not gas. hormuz.now counted 2.3 tanker crossings a day against 47.6 before the crisis, down from 2.9, and no LNG or LPG carrier in seven days. The IRGC's unverified claim that a gas carrier was mined on the southern route on Friday signals that any cargo trying that route is a target. The IEA puts on-land stocks at their lowest since at least 2016. For Taipower the binding variable stays Qatari LNG under force majeure into December. The Taipower and CPC supplementary budget remains unpassed as far as this file knows.
